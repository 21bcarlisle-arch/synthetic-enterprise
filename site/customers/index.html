Two sides of the wall — one household

This is an exhibit, not a customer portal. It puts one household's own view side by side with what the supplier knows about them and what only the simulation knows — three layers that a real supplier's systems keep permanently apart. Nothing here is a login and no real person's data is shown; every account is a modelled household from the run.

Why the sides differ, and why that is the point. A real UK supplier discovers its customers through observable interfaces — meter reads, payments, contacts — and builds estimates on top: cost-to-serve, churn probability, lifetime value. The customer never sees those estimates, and the supplier never sees the simulation's ground truth (a household's actual satisfaction, its causal reaction chain, the wholesale trading margin its consumption produced). That asymmetry is the architectural law this whole project is built on. Showing all three layers at once, each labelled with the side it sits on, is the only way to make it visible.

Relationship to the real portal. Poesys's actual account-holder portal — the customer-facing product surface, with only customer-observable data in it — is a separate system at company/portal/ inside the repository. This page reproduces that portal's view faithfully in the customer's side column and then shows, deliberately, what sits behind it. Choosing “The customer's side” below renders that view on its own.
