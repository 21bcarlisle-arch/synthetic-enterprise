Explore: one customer, end to end

Follow a single household through pricing, consumption, billing and payment — and see, at every stage, the difference between what the world knew and what the company believed.

What this page will show

One customer, followed all the way through, with the wall visible at every step.

  1. Priced — what the supplier offered, and on what evidence.
  2. Chosen — whether the household took it, and what else was available.
  3. Used — what they actually consumed, half-hour by half-hour.
  4. Billed — what the supplier thought they owed, from estimates.
  5. Paid — what arrived, when, and by what method.
  6. Judged — what the supplier believed about the account afterwards.

At every stage it will put two things side by side: what was true in the simulated world, and what the company believed at that moment. Those are different, and the gap between them is the point of the whole project — a real supplier does not know its customers' consumption when it bills them, and neither does this one.

It will also make the change of clock explicit. Consumption happens in half-hours, settlement lands months later, and payment sits somewhere between. That lag is most of what makes energy retail hard, and it is invisible on every page that reports a single number.

Where the world detail lives meanwhile

Explore is where the simulated world gets walked, one household at a time. The wider picture it walks through — the market, the weather, and how prices are actually formed — is on The World, which stays exactly as it was while this page is built.

Why you are seeing this rather than nothing. The structure of this site was put up before every page behind it was written, deliberately. An empty room you can see into is more honest than a door that isn't there, and far more honest than a room furnished with figures we haven't checked.
