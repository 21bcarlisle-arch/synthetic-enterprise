# The Director's Canon

**Version 4, adopted 2026-10-12 on the director's ruling.** He answered the fourteen decisions in §15 with "Agree all". It consolidates version 3 (18 July, the director twin's brief) and every director ruling, canon document, steer and console instruction from June to 12 October 2026: 209 documents and 64 October console entries, reconciled theme by theme. The evidence for every line is in the 12 October canon review: fourteen theme chapters, each ruling cited by id and date, and the ids resolve to their source files.

Quotation marks mark the director's own words, checked against the repository. Text without them is a faithful summary. A line marked *(ruled 12 Oct, decision N)* carries one of those rulings. §15 records all fourteen.

## How this canon works

1. **This file is the canon, and the only one.** "A concept has one home" (31 Aug) applies to canon itself. CLAUDE.md, the model on a page, the operating model, the overview, the pitch and the site quote this file or point to it by id. Where any of them disagrees with it, that page is wrong, and the disagreement is a finding to fix, not a second opinion.
2. **A ruling lands everywhere or it has not landed.** A new canon document carries a "where this lands" list. It is not done until this file and every page on that list carry it, or a page is marked not applicable with a reason. This is R1, consumer-verified completion, applied to canon.
3. **Only the director changes intent.** His words reach this file through staging. A marked factual correction may land from anyone. A superseded line is not deleted. It moves to §16 with what replaced it.
4. **Canon is checked against itself, not only against the code.** The Monday end-to-end check reads this file beside the standing pages and fails on a contradiction, a stale quotation or a superseded line still presented as current.
5. **The twin is retired.** Version 3 was the brief of the director twin, which last acted on 16 July. Its permission code was deleted on 29 July. Version 3 is archived, not edited.

## 1. Mission: what the company is for (M)

**M1. The mission.** "We are creating enterprise value by automating ways to find individual customers we can create value for, and sharing in that value — by saving them money, time and carbon, through personalised modelling, tariffs and advice." *(28 Aug, verbatim; reserved to the director.)*

**M2. Created, then shared.** "Value is created and THEN shared, so every decision has two sides. Transfer is not creation." "Charging someone the cap transfers value rather than creating any." *(28 Aug)*

**M3. Three currencies.** "Money, time and carbon". His plain words for the same three, 5 Oct: "we save customers cash, carbon and convenience". Time means household effort, measured in its own unit (household minutes per kind of activity) and never converted to pounds. *(28 Aug, 5 Sept, 5 Oct; time's unit ruled 12 Oct, decision 3)*

**M4. The method is the asset.** "The enterprise value is the automated METHOD of finding those customers, not the book." The book is the evidence that the method works. *(28 Aug)*

**M5. The thesis.** Decide customer by customer on lifetime value, using only what a real supplier could know. The company plays "like an average player" by default, and beats average only to the degree it understands and predicts the truth behind the simulation better. That means winning "through better prediction and nothing else", "from inference, never from access". Personalisation pays where averaging disappoints and undervalues a customer. Elsewhere a segment is enough. *(June; 25 Aug; 26 Aug; 28 Aug; 7 Sept)*

**M6. How the thesis is judged.** "The fair test compares decisions, not books": for each customer at each renewal, what each rule would have earned on that customer at that moment. "A negative result on the right population is worth more to me than a £3M headline on the wrong one." *(26 Aug; 3 Oct)*

**M7. The heart of the business.** "Payment, credit risk and forward cash forecasting are the heart of this business." *(11 Oct)*

**M8. Fidelity serves the mission.** "Fidelity is not the goal — it is the precondition: a world that cannot press back cannot tell value created from value transferred." The company does not have to die, but it must not be guaranteed to live. Its risks are cash and timing problems, not profit problems. Survival is judged on the domestic book. *(25 July; 28 Aug)*

**M9. The route to reality is narrow.** "You cannot spend any real money. Simulation is different to real money of mine." A fully autonomous licensed supplier is not something the regulator will permit, and "the capped-human design is a research condition, not a licensing plan". Real households would appear only through consenting volunteers with a parallel bill, after a security review. That step is a real-world commitment, reserved to the director and not taken now (ruled 12 Oct, decision 14). *(28 July; 27 Sept)*

**M10. The product brief.** Inertia and reluctance "are not obstacles. They are the operating conditions." Ask for the smallest change, and never ask the same thing twice of someone who has declined. Act at moments of action, not in campaigns. The test is whether this household's cost and carbon actually bent, or whether we merely sent a message. Britain first, global by design. *(28 July)*

**M11. The project's own claim.** Beside the mission sits the project's claim, which is that software can be grown and can maintain itself, rather than only be written. It is the project's claim, not the simulated company's. *(17 Aug)* The front door carries the mission, with this claim beside it (ruled 12 Oct, decision 14).

## 2. Phases: how the project unfolds (V) — record only, no work

*Director, 12 Oct, to the advisor:* "right now we are trying to get to a critical mass of customers and complexity in the universe and supplier. And the associated core functionality to run a supply business. We are using 9y runs with the same data and a small customer group to test and build ... eventually I can imagine wanting to create a more adaptive and learning supplier that builds and fine tunes as it goes. Certainly if we introduce trends and changes. We have to decide or even have mode of continuous simulation or longer term trend runs (EV rise etc) and in these longer runs, and as we are able to grow the customer base we can handle, we want more inflight decision making." He asked for it in the vision and canon, not acted on now.

**V1. Phase 1, Build (now).** Reach a critical mass of world complexity and core supplier functions. Replay the 2016–2025 record, run small books across many seeds, and unpick flat and fixed assumptions. The supplier already decides in flight: a price at each renewal, a save when the loss notice lands, collections as payments fail. Its policies and models stay fixed within a run, though. The builder improves them between runs.

**V2. Phase 2, Adapt.** The supplier learns within a run, from its own observables and only at the point in time: retraining on a schedule, updating its policies, running its own experiments. Success means beating an identical supplier frozen at the start of the run.

**V3. Phase 3, Forward and continuous.** Runs go past the record, with trends and regime changes nobody designed for: EVs, heat pumps, solar, new tariffs, prices, climate. Horizons get longer, a continuous mode appears, and the book is larger. This is step 6 of the priority order (P1) once the levers exist.

**V4. What the phases ask of today's work.** Build nothing that assumes policies are loaded once per run. Keep every decision callable at any date, from that date's state. Record what the supplier knew when it decided. Treat the live supplier's shape as the destination: each day's events processed per account, with state kept. "Build nothing that forecloses forward simulation." *(5 Oct)*

**V5. The risk of staying in Phase 1.** Replaying the same history means the builder knows the future: 2022, the switching collapse, the price path. That is look-ahead at the level of the designer, the wall problem one level up. More seeds vary the households but not the shared prices and weather. Forward runs with trends the builder has not seen are the real test.

## 3. The world and the wall (W)

**W1. The wall and its test.** The company "may only know what a real UK supplier could know". Ask of every line: could a real UK energy supplier have known this, at that moment? "Ground truth never crosses; observables do." `company/interfaces/sim_interface.py` is the seam and the only route. The wall is not relaxed for any epoch or for book growth, and a run "must never look ahead". *(June; 12 July; 28 Aug; 9 Oct)*

**W2. The gap is the product.** "The world creates customer behaviour behind the curtain, from evidence. The supplier sees only what a real supplier could see, and problem-solves against it. The supplier's value is in closing that gap, so it must not be designed from knowledge of what's behind the curtain." *(11 Oct)* The cost of imperfect information, belief against truth, is a standing metric. *(June)*

**W3. Hidden state is drawn as state and found by behaviour.** Each household is a public prior, guessable from an address, plus a private residual found from the meter shape, the payment record, contacts and asking. Cohort labels, green stance, price sensitivity and channel preference stay unreadable across the seam. A trait with no observable channel either gets one or is marked hidden. Vulnerability is "a hidden household state rather than by rule". Complaints are "a floor on dissatisfaction, not a measure of it", so the world models the silent unhappy. *(27 July; 5 Sept; 5 Oct; 8 Oct)* How far responsiveness may correlate with signals a supplier holds is open, and the machine owes a proposal (§15, owed item 4).

**W4. The wall runs both ways, and latency is physics.** "A bill is outbound, never an observable". The inbound observable is the payment or complaint that answers the bill. Settlement arrives months late. A payment failure off direct debit is a detection latency with a measured distribution: "Do not compress the lag to zero to make the number look better." *(23 July; 25 July)*

**W5. Beliefs and forecasts belong to the company.** Recency, sentiment and adaptive expectations are properties of the company's belief formation, modelled company-side and scored. No downstream consumer decides on outturn. Forecast error is the natural wall on the future. The company holds one consumption forecast per property, the best against held-back actuals, used by purchasing, direct debit setting, unbilled estimation and customer value. *(22–23 July; 11–12 Oct)*

**W6. The seam is the only route, and the company has no route to the real world.** `company/` and `saas/` "may not open a socket, ever, directly or transitively — refused by construction, not by approval." The simulation drawing on real-world sources "isn't a loophole, it's the job". Bytes the harness fetches may become documentation, design records and knowledge, "never a runtime input the company reads". *(17–18 Aug; 28 Aug)* The wall is designed as the go-live seam: moving from a simulated endpoint to a real one should be an integration task, not a rewrite. The Transition Test is a design test of this seam, not a go-live plan (ruled 12 Oct, decision 14).

**W7. The four principles of 11 October.**
1. *World first:* "build the household side before the supplier side ... Build it from research, with no reference to any supplier policy. Where evidence is missing, carry a labelled range, not a number."
2. *Freeze, then solve:* "Calibrate and fix the world's behaviour, with its predictions filed, before testing any supplier solution against it. Don't change the world after seeing supplier results unless new evidence requires it, and say so when you do."
3. *Separate the work:* "World behaviour and supplier policy are separate items in separate lanes. The supplier side reads only through the seam, and supplier strategies are graded on fresh seeds and across the world's uncertainty ranges, so they can't be tuned to one value."
4. *Truth stays an instrument:* "Tools that ask the world directly (the decision probe, the holdout truth) are for grading only, never inputs to a supplier decision."

**W8. Unpublished behaviour is a labelled range, checked and never fed.** Customer-chosen options with no published take-up run "only as sensitivities across a take-up range, never as findings". Published outcome rates are "a reality check on the world rather than an input", compared with definitions matched. *(8–12 Oct)*

**W9. Change the book, not the world.** "Change the book, not the world, and not the measurement." Segment choice belongs to the company's acquisition decision, not to the world's draw. *(26 and 30 Aug)*

**W10. Independent anchors.** Generation and validation take their anchors from disjoint sources. The company never validates against simulation truth. *(20–23 July)*

**W11. No personal microdata.** "Published aggregates and cross-tabs only. The ratified pitch stakes a public claim that no household data has entered the system; that must stay true." *(20 July)*

**W12. The uncommon are over-represented.** Customers who get suboptimal treatment in a real book precisely because they are uncommon "are over-represented rather than rounded away". *(7 Sept)*

## 4. Building the world (F)

**F1. The simulation leads, and the world must press.** "The SIM leads and the company catches up." A world that cannot press back cannot tell value created from value transferred (M8). World work continues "only where it blocks a supplier result". Where it does block one, it goes first (W7). *(June; 28 Aug; 10–11 Oct)*

**F2. Aggregates are never targets; generation is bottom-up.** "Every household carries its own behaviour and its own probabilities, derived from its own attributes with a hidden rationale". No macro variable is ever set as a target. "Published figures are checks the world must pass, never dials that set it." Clamping an aggregate to pass a check "passes the test by destroying the thing the test exists to protect". Failures repair downward, never sideways. *(23 July; 31 Aug; 3 Sept)*

**F3. The validation ladder.** Rung 0 is red lines. Rung 1 is level: "a world whose level sits on the exact edge of the band every year has almost certainly been fitted to that edge". Rung 2 is mechanism: "a mechanism the world must obey, not a number it must match". Rung 3 is heterogeneity: "this rung is the thesis itself". Checks are red tests, not advisory bands. *(31 Aug)*

**F4. Which world is the director's (R13).** "The world changes only for fidelity reasons, decided blind to company results. Which world the company lives through is the director's, named and versioned in a file he can read." "We want resilience and the capability to endure, not pot luck in one world." A change to any published marginal is a fidelity finding, decided blind to profit, never a side effect. Plain factual corrections to the world are made and reported, unless the fix changes something he ruled (G6). *(12 July; 23 July; 5 Sept; 8–9 Oct)*

**F5. A person, not a segment.** "The bill payer, not the meter." "The person who doesn't pay their bill in February isn't necessarily a bad debt risk. They might have just had a baby." Until a trait changes a behaviour, calling it coupled overstates what exists. "A household that has never switched at any price is disengaged, not price-insensitive." *(June; 27–30 Aug; 5 Sept)*

**F6. The market must contest.** Choice and channel are the missing mechanic. With no competitor, no channel, no quote and no reason attached to a departure, "the company can never be right or wrong about why a household moved". Beating a flat baseline in a market that cannot react compares two internal policies, not two suppliers. *(28–30 Aug)*

**F7. Tails, not means.** "The fidelity score is the worst-explained cell of that grid, not the population average." "Annual averages hide tails. Measure it at the extremes." Sampling leans to the tails: "a world's unlikeliness discounts its EV contribution but never excuses dying in it". Robustness, commercial value and survival are scored separately, never conflated. *(19–25 July; 5–9 Sept)*

**F8. Anchored, evidenced, believable.** Every assumption is registered against an external source. Where the simulation diverges from a published benchmark, "the divergence is recorded rather than tuned away". The bar is whether it feels like the real UK market to a 20-year veteran. "Theorise where the physics is solid ... Don't theorise where it's behaviour." *(19–22 July; 5–8 Sept)*

**F9. No interim tuning.** "No interim tuning" while structural work waits. "The naive baseline must be FROZEN and independent". An aggregate lift is never stated without the per-cell truth beside it. *(19–28 July)*

**F10. Assumptions are hypotheses.** Fusion and coupling assumptions are testable hypotheses, each naming the observation that would refute it. "An honest gap is recoverable; a confident error is not." No pair of traits is drawn independently without a register entry saying so. *(20–23 July; 5–7 Sept)*

**F11. Measurement habits.** "Before measuring a thing, say what it is." "Before dividing two numbers, say out loud what each one counts." "A prediction filed after the answer is not a prediction." "Identical to four decimals" is a tell: check the mechanism, not the number. "Don't build until you've told me the shape." **The tie-breaker:** when the evidence is ambiguous, "erring against our own thesis is the safe direction". *(27 Aug to 9 Sept)*

**F12. Independent reconstruction.** "Arithmetic checked only against itself is not checked." "The remedy is not more tests written by the same hand. It is independent reconstruction". Every disagreement is a finding about the biller, and "Where it disagrees, the validator wins." *(12 Aug; 2 Sept; 5 Oct)*

**F13. The synthetic book.** "The sample is modelled houses, each placed in a coherent weather cell, with a coherent set of people living in it. Real data calibrates and checks it; real data is not the thing we select from." Each life is a fresh draw from the fitted joint, never a fixed library: "The fitted joint is the asset; the houses are not." Draw for difference, not for proportion. "Coverage is a property of the ensemble of runs". *(20 July; 5–7 Sept)*

**F14. The demand vector.** "A household is a vector, not a number, and the sample has to span the range of those vectors across the stock, including the tails, not match their averages." Cases are chosen for difference and the weights carry representativeness. Report both numbers: the N that reproduces the observed distribution, and the N that also spans intervention response. Treat the second as the real one. *(7 Sept)*

**F15. Weather cells: one shared world.** Divide the UK into clusters with similar primary weather drivers, at "the maximum level of simplicity that can be achieved whilst still capturing fidelity". Model persistence and cross-cell synchrony ("when Glasgow is cold, is London?"). The count is derived, not designed. "Two households in the same cell must experience identical weather — that's what makes the difference in their demand attributable to fabric and people." *(5–20 Sept)*

**F16. The half-hourly spine stays half-hourly.** GB settlement is half-hourly, "and that is not an implementation detail, it is the market". "Per-household shape is not an enhancement to the demand work; it is the deliverable." For gas, "the resolution is needed for the PHYSICS". *(24 Aug; 7–8 Sept)*

**F17. Fundamentals plus an honest residual.** Model the fundamentals. Generate the residual "as a calibrated stochastic process with honest tails and regime behaviour; never pretend to forecast the unforecastable". "A stationary, tame weather world makes every downstream claim soft." *(22–27 July)*

**F18. Two layers kept separate.** Physical demand and commercial behaviour stay separate. "Merged, we cannot tell a household that used less because nobody was home from one that could not afford it." The gap between rated and in-use performance is modelled, not ignored. *(5–7 Sept)*

**F19. The book is earned.** "A growth curve that cannot be lost is not a growth curve." "The book stays earned, never granted." Thin cells are a finding, not a defect to paper over. *(25 July; 11–17 Aug)*

**F20. Facts not to re-litigate.**
- Generate the type from behaviour, and let the published split fall out as a check.
- 2022 was a price effect, not scarcity, and "2022 is a fact, not a scenario".
- Model what the household is shown, not only what it would pay.
- Acquisition and churn do not share one elasticity.
- A single-fuel book distorts every per-customer number.
- "There's no such thing as a half-month direct debit."
- The bill itself shocks nobody: for a direct debit household the shock is the payment change.
- "A company misreading the law stays structurally possible."

*(12 July to 1 Sept)*

**F21. Real behaviour, checked like for like (October).**
- Gaps against published rates are checked with definitions matched: leaving the supplier against leaving the tariff, any amount behind against the survey's measure.
- A real gap is fixed as a factual correction, unless the fix changes a ruling. The results that depend on it are then re-graded.
- The world is handed the discounted rate, not a flat modifier.
- Acquisition is spread through the year as real switching is, so that renewals stop landing on the same days.
- Rare events are built in this order: voids tied to move-outs, then vulnerability as a hidden state, then theft as a short study.

*(3 Oct; 8–9 Oct)*

## 5. Knowledge first (K)

**K1. Knowledge first is a rule.** "A number you need is a question to research, never a value to pick." The order is published sources, then discovery against what we have built, then a roadmap. Where the record runs out, say so. The code "carries the gap explicitly rather than a placeholder that looks like an answer." *(28–31 Aug)*

**K2. Omission is not neutrality.** Where physics or strong logic makes a relationship near-certain, model it as a stated belief rather than leave it out. "Omission is not neutrality — it asserts zero, and zero is usually the one value we know is wrong." "A parameter we can move is worth more than a number we're confident in." *(8 Sept. This corrects an over-reading of K1.)*

**K3. Unpublished means a ranged toggle.** "Where the truth isn't published, make it an assumption toggle: a parameter with a plausible range, and test whether the answer changes across it. If it doesn't change any decision, the question doesn't matter. If it does, dig harder — and only bring it to me if it's both decision-changing and unresolvable." Ranges come from published data and are labelled as estimates. *(5–11 Oct)*

**K4. The practitioner comes last.** Knowledge has three sides: published evidence, discovery of what our code does, and a practitioner for what is too obvious to be written down. His corrections are "the practitioner starting point, not the answer." "When a question touches regulation, search Ofgem's licence conditions and decisions before asking me." "A question to me comes only after that page shows the answer isn't published." *(1 Sept; 6–11 Oct)*

**K5. Scope first, from the domain.** Write the scope brief first, independently of what we hold: what must a complete treatment of this topic contain? Then assemble. "The DELTA between brief and assembly is the primary finding." *(19–28 July)*

**K6. The machine owns the design; his guesses are hypotheses.** "If a question can be settled by running something, run it." His ideas are "My guess, to test rather than assume" and "If one of mine is wrong, say so." *(28 Aug; 26 Sept; 3 Oct)*

**K7. Knowledge is the product.** Understanding lands in the knowledge layer with its sources and its gaps stated. Pages are written for an outsider, who should learn something rather than check us, because "the honest version is more persuasive than a clean one". Owed pages get written. Parked work is written up so that its findings survive. *(30 Aug to 4 Oct)*

**K8. Knowledge before build.** "Before building or changing any supplier capability, write or amend the relevant knowledge page first: what the rules require, what published practice and evidence say, what's unknown (labelled GAP), and which choices the design makes and why. Publish it on the site, and cite the page in the build commit." *(11 Oct, standing)*

**K9. A page's shape and place.** A page says "what the published evidence establishes, what it does not, what we have assumed and why, and what would settle it". A page that is mostly its own to-do list ships as a stub. Topics split on one test: shared material is one topic, different material is separate topics joined by a typed edge. "Amend an existing page wherever one fits; start a new one only when nothing does." A page's headline can never disagree with its title. *(28 July to 11 Oct)*

**K10. Staleness and claim status are visible.** Every page carries a last-reviewed date and shows "review due" when it is past due. A figure that the pitch or the site cites must be derivable from primary state at publication time. *(28 July; 17–18 Aug)*

**K11. The published record governs the numbers.** Fetch real rates rather than extrapolating, and mark anything still extrapolated where it is shown. "The commons wins": a document is "the origin of the question, not the authority on the answer". Prefer a published series to a rebuild. *(14 Aug to 8 Oct)*

**K12. Constants carry their origin.** Every domain constant in `company/` and `saas/` carries one of three origins: a citation; a labelled belief that something grades; or a named simplification saying what it would take to do properly. The same name may not carry two values anywhere. "Simplify wherever necessary — but record what was simplified, what it would take to do properly, and what it would change if done." *(28–31 Aug)*

## 6. What comes first (P)

**P1. The order from here (5 Oct), superseding the 4 September re-ranking where they differ.**
1. Knowledge, now and wide, in parallel with everything else.
2. Unbilled energy and billing accuracy: "This finishes billing correctly."
3. Forward CLV, "the spine every lever is judged against", built and tested on held-back history.
4. Per-customer decisions, continuing alongside.
5. The levers, in merit order: debt cashflow forecasting and negotiating tools; home moves; next best action; margin and risk; value-add.
6. Forward simulation, "a hypothesis that longer runs add value", taken after the levers deliberately.
7. Communications, sentiment and NPS, last.

*Amended the same day:* next best action is one decision spanning debt, retention, upsell and carbon advice, judged by CLV (C7). *Since 11–12 Oct:* steps 3 and 5 run through one payment and debt engine on a forward cash forecast (C9). Its order is the four-principles audit, then the household payment world, then the supplier engine, then one consumption forecast.

**P2. The order changes by evidence.** "Doing what this document says after learning it is wrong is the failure, not departing from it." When knowledge work changes the order, the machine proposes the change with the evidence and keeps going. The director rules.

**P3. Sequencing within a step uses four tests:** "whether other work rests on it; where it sits in the merit order (pays, stays, margin, value-add); whether the world contains what it needs yet; and whether the knowledge exists."

**P4. The draw follows the order.** "Verify the draw follows this order, not just the dials." On 4 September a re-ranking changed the weights and the work did not move. In-flight work finishes first. The check reports and never scores. It is direction, not a target (H1, Law A; ruled 12 Oct, decision 11).

**P5. A supplier-weighted portfolio.** "My priority order is mostly supplier work." Every stretch carries "at least two supplier items from the priority order". World fidelity continues "only where it blocks a supplier result", and where it blocks one it goes first (W7). Spread the work across knowledge, world, supplier and interfaces rather than letting one area absorb the week. Each stretch log reports the world, supplier and other split of commits. *(3 Oct; 10 Oct)*

**P6. Product first.** Product is what a real supplier or a real market would have. Machinery exists only because a harness builds this. The website publishes product but is neither. "Machinery only when nothing can land." Machinery is drawn only on breakage, or as a named dependency of a product item, stated as such in the commit. The test for any day is what a customer or a domain reader would notice. *(23 July; 5 Sept; 16 Sept; 3 Oct)*

**P7. Breakage and wrongness go first.** Something wrong on a live surface, or a crash, goes first. "Landings come before experiments." "When your own machinery blocks you, fix the scheduling and carry on. Stop only for decisions reserved to me." *(9 Aug to 9 Oct)*

**P8. Priority is his; sequencing is the machine's.** A list "is not a sequence and must not be executed as a list". "Sequence it all yourself and start. Don't come back for permission on the shape." *(16 July to 3 Oct)*

**P9. Method for large work.** Measure the ceiling before the programme. Large programmes start with a proposal. "Nothing is deleted until its replacement is live." "Cost is the governor." *(11 Aug to 4 Sept)*

**P10. Look ahead only for constraints on the present.** Forward discovery is ranked by certainty and by how critical it is to the mission. Contingent policy is one line, never a build. *(19–21 July)*

**P11. Book size and composition are his.** The founder ceiling and the prospect cap: "Those two numbers stay my decision." Since 8 Oct: founders 400 (ceiling 500) and prospect cap 400, with the count to be set from the 400-founder run. I&C stays suspended from the book, kept built and tested. *(24 Aug; 8 Oct)*

**P12. He gives the reading; the machine paces itself.** *His words, 12 Oct, installed with v4 and replacing the drafted P12:* "you manage your own pace. I'll tell you my weekly usage reading from time to time. Use it with tools/tick_burn_rate.py to work out what each kind of work costs you in my points, and pace yourself to land at about 95% at the reset. Never wait or idle to save budget. Pace by what you choose and how you run it: cheaper items, smaller turns, the right model tier, fewer sessions running side by side. Lengthen the tick cadence only as a last resort, and never stop. If you're behind the line, go harder." *(25 Sept to 11 Oct; ruled 12 Oct, decision 12; amended in his words the same day)*

**P13. Recurring waste is a class.** "When a class of wasted work keeps recurring, ask why it recurs, not only how to clear this instance — and if you can't answer because nothing measures it, the missing measurement is the first thing to build." Runtime state such as heartbeats and liveness lives in the ops repository, not in main. "Don't patch it again." *(30 Sept; 4 Oct)*

**P14. Mothball cleanly; triage what is carried.** When a section is mothballed, stop the work that feeds it. Parked work keeps its findings, worktree path and state on record. "Count distinct items, not rows." Triage every carried item once: fix it, fold it into an existing class register, or accept it with a stated reason and retire it. An item carried unchanged for a week must be triaged rather than copied forward again. *(19 Aug; 27 Sept; 7 Oct)*

## 7. The customer and the money (C)

**C1. Every output is a diagnostic, never a target.** "R12 anti-goal-seek — margin is a diagnostic, never a target", generalised to every output. *(12 July; 28 Aug)*

**C2. A performance claim passes the wall, beats a baseline and carries its null.** "The advantage must come from inference, never from access." The control is a supplier applying flat rules with no per-customer view. A belief-against-truth gap is a measurement, never evidence of skill, and is never stated without its null interval. Say "we cannot tell" when that is the result. Compare decisions, not books (M6). Report founders and campaign wins separately, or show the mix does not change the answer. *(25 Aug; 31 Aug; 3 Oct; 10 Oct)*

**C3. The pool is not the book.** "The company's book is what it earned". No £ figure from the curriculum cohort leads a page: show £ per customer, rates and distributions, with N. *(23–27 July)*

**C4. Segments earn their place by decisions.** Segments rank on what they teach the company, "not which customers are profitable". A segment scores value only if a decision is conditioned on it. Protection is a floor outside the ranking (X3). Cohorts are combinations of needs, attitudes and behaviours, with the fewest segments that cover the valuable variance. *(19–29 July)*

**C5. The CLV merit order: pays, stays, margin, value-add.** Value is contribution times survival. Where contribution is negative, a longer life multiplies the loss. "Good management of renters and won't pay (but can) can create as much value ... as other value add." The merit order fixes the build order and breaks ties. Each decision itself is judged by forward CLV (C7). Pricing must see who pays: payment history is "the first rung of my CLV merit order". *(5 Sept; 3–5 Oct; 10 Oct)*

**C6. CLV done properly.** CLV runs on three horizons: expected, actual, and re-forecast continuously. Systematic variance is model error, reported as pricing accuracy by cohort. Every action is an expected-value choice against doing nothing, not a rule. The CLV discount rate and terminal-value method are still to be chosen (§15, owed item 9). *(June; 5 Sept)*

**C7. Next best action is one decision per customer.** "The goal is tailored next best action per customer — their benefit first, then ours." Debt management, retention, upsell and carbon advice are all next best actions, "judged by CLV, rather than as a fixed ranking of measures". It ranks on joint value, the household's benefit plus our forward value, with a no-loss floor on both sides (ruled 12 Oct, decision 5). Whether the company's overall score becomes two-sided is a later ruling. The build order is the reactive save, then home-mover retention, then debt as next best action. *(5 Oct; 10 Oct)*

**C8. Retention is save and win-back.** "GB retention is mainly save and win-back activity on the loss notice, plus retention of home movers. Blanket renewal discounts are not the lever." Rates come from the switching process's own published data, as ranged toggles: "a reality check on the world rather than an input". Test the shapes, and say if the answer turns on a number. *(8 Oct)* The company may learn from published trials and from its own randomised holdout, never from the world's truth (ruled 12 Oct, decision 7).

**C9. The payment and debt engine.**
- One engine, centred on "a forward cash forecast per customer: expected bills from the one consumption forecast, expected payments and their probability given payment method and track record".
- It drives:
  - the provision, as expected loss per customer, replacing the flat assumption;
  - limits on how far each balance may drift;
  - the personal payment plan;
  - early warnings before debt builds;
  - automated self-serve responses within those limits, "where the forecast says some money steadily beats none".
- "Credit risk sets how far a customer's balance may drift from zero, not how much they pay over the year." Low-risk customers get smoothing, prompt refund of surplus credit and no catch-up shocks. Higher-risk customers are kept near zero without winter bill shock.
- "Human handling is kept for vulnerability and exceptions."
- A fixed direct debit rests on the best current information and is reassessed when new information would change it materially (SLC 27.15).
- Supplier-controlled levers come first. Customer-chosen options come later, as sensitivities. The portfolio ceiling on credit held stays.

*(11–12 Oct)*

**C10. Provision honestly; debt follows the person.** The gap between provision and realised loss is measured and published, never tuned away: "a company that believes it provisions perfectly is a wider hidden gap wearing a better number." Estimates are on billed, outcomes on settled, cash on banked. Debt misattributed at a change of tenancy is a customer-harm event: debt follows the person, not the property. *(25 July; 6–9 Oct)*

**C11. Wholesale machinery prices forecast error.** The annualised shaped energy cost is the benchmark. The product ladder is real and the term structure moves. The hedge programme is scored against the benchmark net of friction. *(22–23 July)*

**C12. Growth is a cash risk.** "λ=1.0 is the zero-effort floor, not a law of nature." Level direct debits build summer credit, "a Ponzi in all but name, and it holds only while growth continues". Acquisition spend that drives collateral demands is what would make survival mean something. *(25 July; 28 Aug)*

**C13. Price is formed, never asserted.** "I really want pricing to be right in the end, not just look right now." Power must be "substantially reconstructible from gas, carbon, demand and wind on ordinary days — or it was not formed, only generated." Pricing follows expected cost, default risk and collections included, "and let the answer emerge rather than imposing a floor". The rules on what pricing may do come from cited sources, "not my recollection and not yours". *(22–25 July; 25 Aug)*

**C14. Corrections and proposals.** "Where a gap is real, fix it as a factual correction, unless the fix changes something I ruled, in which case bring me the proposal". *(8 Oct)* Numbers that stay his: the founder ceiling, the prospect cap, and when a rule starts to bind. The capital target applies from 31 March 2025 at £115 per dual-fuel account. Before the July 2023 decision there is none; between the decision and the binding date it is a plan-ahead target the company may choose to meet (ruled 12 Oct, decision 13).

## 8. Fairness and protection (X)

**X1. Cost is not loaded onto a group because it can push back less.** The 23 September ruling, as he corrected it: "turning customers down on payment behaviour, credit or arrears is ordinary practice, but shifting cost onto prepayment customers isn't." "The principle is that cost is not loaded onto a group because it is less able or less likely to push back." The earlier paraphrase, "a price keyed to the meter may not", is wrong and is to be fixed "wherever it's recorded". *(8 Oct)*

**X2. Save offers and renewal prices.** "A save offer made on a loss notice may vary by acquisition route or time on the default tariff. Renewal prices may not." "A save offer must never be paid for by raising the price of customers who stay". This is shown by checking stayers' prices with and without save offers. SLC 22B and its retention carve-out are to be checked as still in force. *(8 Oct)* Ruled 12 Oct (decision 6): a renewal price may reflect that customer's expected cost, payment history included, within the ability-to-pay duties and the rule that vulnerable customers are treated at least as well. It may not vary by acquisition route or time on the default tariff. A pre-expiry offer is a renewal price until a loss notice exists.

**X3. Protection is a floor outside the ranking.** "Protection is a floor outside the ranking, not a term inside it." No metric can demote a regulatorily significant group (vulnerability, fuel poverty, prepayment, priority services, debt) below full-fidelity modelling. "Vulnerable customers must be treated at least as well as anyone else in the same position." Vulnerable and stretched households are reported separately. Location data is opt-in and never set back on inference alone for anyone who may be vulnerable. Gamification must not be regressive. *(20 July; 6 Sept; 8–11 Oct)*

**X4. Ability to pay is a hard constraint.** A repayment mechanism without an affordability assessment is non-compliant by construction (SLC 27.5–27.8). A fixed direct debit "must be set on the best and most current information in all cases ... and reassessed when new information would change it materially." *(25 July; 11 Oct)*

**X5. Harm is priced, bounded and signed.** "R = 8:1 SIGNED. Band 5:1–10:1", changed only by his explicit re-signature. Harm is heavy-tailed and scales with vulnerability. Licence loss is a scenario, not a probability. Hedging stays "within a director-set risk envelope", and the 2021-style tail must be survivable by construction. "At even odds, ANY harm-aversion at all means forbear. Pursuit must be EARNED by confidence." *(13 July; 6 Sept)*

**X6. Margin stays off the front door; no value-based pricing at quote.** Acquisition spend and service design may be value-based. The price quoted may not. *(20 Aug; 6 Sept)*

**X7. What serves the customer is not always what the market has normalised.** "The gap between true need and market norm is itself a modellable phenomenon and arguably where the product opportunity sits." The personal payment plan is its first home. *(25 July; 11 Oct)*

**X8. Don't price comfort, floor it.** An 18°C health floor: below it, the fabric needs work, not the thermostat. This is parked as second order, not revoked. *(8 Sept)*

## 9. Carbon (CO)

**CO1. What carbon is for.** "Its main job is to show customers what they've saved and could save in CO2, so those motivated by it can enjoy it. That clarifies the mission's carbon part rather than changing it". Carbon is one of three currencies, not demoted. £/tCO₂e is an internal diagnostic, not the public headline (ruled 12 Oct, decision 4). *(28 Aug; 5 Oct)*

**CO2. Savings are usage-side only, never discounts.** Bills are cut by reducing or shifting use, never by discounting. "A discount can be gamed and a kilowatt-hour cannot." Save and retention offers are price transfers and never count as carbon or customer savings. *(28 July)*

**CO3. The score is a diagnostic, kept in three ledgers.** £/tCO₂e equals (cost to serve + cost to persuade, compute included) divided by carbon abated, against £273/t. "It is a diagnostic, not a target". "Three ledgers, all reported: carbon saved for customers; carbon spent serving them — people, compute, tokens; and the net position. A carbon claim that counts only one side is not a claim." *(28 July)*

**CO4. Published with provenance.** "Publish it with its provenance, and say plainly what it does and does not yet include." Household emissions are measured. Abatement is not yet measured, because nothing supplies the counterfactual. That is the missing piece of the "saved" display in CO1. Transmission and distribution losses go on their own named line beside NESO's figure, at DESNZ's published factor, never folded into the grid series (ruled 12 Oct, decision 4). *(25 Aug; 5–9 Oct)*

**CO5. Intensity: published history, modelled futures.** "NESO publishes half-hourly carbon intensity for GB, and it's what a real supplier would use for any customer offer. I'm happy with a correlated estimate — it doesn't need to be precise." For history, align the published series to settlement and fill pre-coverage gaps simply. For futures, "fit a statistical model of carbon intensity on that history". EP13 is parked: "Don't lose EP13's findings ... Write it up, park the atom". *(4 Oct)*

**CO6. Carbon knowledge is balanced and feeds next best action.** "For a typical gas-heated home, heating may be around five times the carbon of all its electricity." Test, don't accept:
- an EV is a large total saving but raises a household's electricity carbon;
- solar is now mostly a money decision;
- a heat pump is often carbon-led.

Map each measure as win-win, carbon-led or money-led. That map is the basis for next best action. *(5 Oct)*

## 13. Running the simulation (R)

**R1. The budget is the design.** Human input, money, tokens and carbon are all deliberately capped. Capped human input forces genuine autonomy, and capped cost forces architectural efficiency. Unspent allowance is wasted. "You cannot spend any real money." *(28 July; 6 Sept; 27 Sept)*

**R2. Work is the default; rest is the defect.** When the machine has nothing to do, the work exists but cannot be seen, or nothing is authorised to mint it. Rest is indefensible while any published register holds an open item. Judge investigation by whether it converges on a change: "an hour of looking that ends in two lines is exactly right; a seventh pass that ends in another document is not." *(19 July to 24 Aug)*

**R3. Director instructions and severe alarms draw first.** Director-authored acts are rung zero. A severe alarm pre-empts the draw. *(27 July to 17 Aug)*

**R4. Every run is accountable and reproducible.** Every run emits a ledger row. Seeding comes from one canonical random stream. "The same seed must give the same book" before and after every engineering change. Identical inputs regenerate nothing. *(8 July to 28 July; 8–9 Oct)*

**R5. Why we run (8 Oct).** "Every run should answer a stated question with the smallest run that can answer it":
- seconds for one household or one rule;
- minutes for the probe, the decision set, or a few hundred homes over a year or two;
- hours for a full book over the decade.

A full book is justified only for rare events that need many customer-years to appear, for book-level money (provisioning, hedge, cash, price cap), and for the periodic end-to-end check. "Any run over an hour records its question before launch", says why the minutes tier cannot answer it, and says what result would change what we do.

**R6. How we run (October).**
- The fidelity engine builds and calibrates the world. Lean decision sets and the per-decision probe run the experiments: "nine times the signal at a tenth of the cost".
- Seed families: four 40-founder books side by side on distinct seeds under the box budget, with debt read as the share of homes behind and value at home level. Founders and wins are reported separately.
- Founders-only books answer back-book questions.
- "The 400-founder book stays the production shape and the periodic end-to-end check."
