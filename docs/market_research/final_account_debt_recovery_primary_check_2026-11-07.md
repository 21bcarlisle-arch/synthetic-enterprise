## Notes on the verdicts

**A. DRS 75% is not a live-debt figure.** The working paper averages "Closed or Live" accounts and "all payment methods", for crisis-era debt (accrued 1 Apr 2022 to 31 Mar 2024). The pack's table labels rows 6-7 "live". That label is not supported. The working paper also says closed accounts are mostly excluded from Phase 1 ("All other closed accounts will be excluded from phase 1", para 3.20) but closed accounts where the customer is actively engaging to repay are eligible. The pack's statement "Live accounts only... Final-account debt is not addressed" was taken from the impact assessment definition and is too strong against the working paper.

**B. What the Centrica final-bill figure does and does not say.** A final-bills allowance of 84% on a book where more than 90% of the balance (485 of 537) is over 90 days old means that Centrica expects to collect roughly 16p of each £ of gross final-bill debt on the balance outstanding at the year end. That is a conditional figure for the stock, not the loss on a cohort at departure. Debt that has already been paid, or written off in earlier years (£74m residential in 2025), is not in the denominator. The cohort lifetime loss at departure could be higher or lower. Do not read 84% as an LGD at the moment of leaving.

**C. Recoveries after write-off.** Group recoveries of previously written-off receivables were £3m (2025) and £10m (2024), against write-offs of £135m and £160m. The ratio is 2.2% and 6.3%. These are two flows in one year, from different cohorts, so the ratio is only a rough order of magnitude. It does say that post-write-off recovery at Centrica is a small number, in the low single digits of the written-off amount per year.

**D. The SPERL and Octopus coverage figures are whole-book, not final-account.** The 35.7% and 30.6% blend direct-debit customers (low loss) with credit and final customers. They fit Centrica's whole-book 44%, and the pack's use of them as "book coverage" is fair. They cannot support any final-account rate. They are also inconsistent with a final-account rate of 100%, or of 35%.

## What this means for the write-off rule

**Pack numbers that survive at grade P**
- Whole-book allowance coverage: SPERL 35.7% (2024) and 32.0% (2023); Octopus 30.6% (Apr 2022) and 34.3% (Apr 2021, as a comparative). Centrica 44% (billed), 38% (Retail gross). (Pack rows 1-13 cite coverage of 30-36% as the basis.)
- The "lost (final)" customer segment exists in a supplier's provision matrix, so suppliers do not treat it as an automatic 100% loss (SPERL Note 2(d)(i)).
- DRS weighted provisioning of ~75% (mixed Closed and Live), floor 56.7%, net reimbursement ~20% and up to ~38%; collection cost 3p per £1 and average recovery time of more than 22 months (DRS impact assessment, para 3.10 and its footnotes 6-7; page not pinned); 12.2% working-capital rate (same).
- SLC 14.4 transfer objection for unpaid Outstanding Charges demanded in writing 28+ days previously; SLC 14.6 £500 ceiling.

**New primary evidence the pack lacked**
- Centrica final-bill provision coverage of 84% in 2025 (85.6% in 2024), against 44.7% for pay-on-receipt-of-bill and 2.8% for direct debit. This directly brackets the pack's rows 9-13 (35% to 100% by age) and its blended LGD of 65-80%. A primary supplier figure for final bills sits above that range, at about 84-88% for an aged final-bill stock.
- A primary price for assigned prepayment debt: 90% of ex-VAT plus full VAT.

**Pack numbers that do not survive, or need correcting**
- DAP lower bound £10 is wrong. It is £20 (REC v3.1).
- "Assigned debt = 0% loss to the losing supplier" is wrong. It is about 10% of the ex-VAT amount, paid within 28 days of invoice.
- The "30 working days to pay" attached to every blocked switch is wrong. It applies to the unilateral-variation case only.
- "Live LGD ~75%" for 2-3-year-old debt: the 75% is a mixed Live and Closed figure.

**GAP, with no primary source found**
- A recovery rate on final (closed) accounts, by age or at departure.
- Debt-sale prices (pence per £) for GB domestic energy debt, fresh or aged.
- DCA recovery curves and commission rates.
- Repayment-plan break rate, the final-bill-to-write-off timeline beyond Centrica's "six to twelve months from the issuance of a final bill" default definition, and the Scottish 5-year prescription period (not checked).
- Any final-account rate by age from SPERL, Octopus or any other supplier (SPERL discloses the segmentation, not the numbers).

**Consequence for the rule.** A 100% write-off of every failed bill for any leaver is contradicted at grade P by the segment's existence in SPERL's matrix, and by Centrica's own 84% coverage on final bills. Centrica's figure is, however, closer to 100% than the pack's 65-80% LGD. The evidence supports "high, not total, loss on aged final-bill stock". It does not support 65-80% at departure, and it gives no primary timing curve. A world parameter built on rows 14-22 would rest on grade A alone. Any such parameter should be carried as a stated belief with a range, labelled GAP. Candidate anchor: final-bill stock loss of about 84-88% (Centrica 2025), plus a small post-write-off recovery tail of low single digits per year of the written-off amount (note C, rough), with PPM debt up to £500 assigned at 90% ex-VAT where the switch rules apply.
