**Knowledge:** how-households-behave-toward-a-supplier

**Written 2026-11-21** by the delivery seat, for the director's 21 Nov 07:23 steer
(`docs/staging/advisor/done/DIRECTOR_STEER_THE_CUSTOMER_VOICE_IS_A_STANDING_KNOWLEDGE_SOURCE_TRAWLED_FOR_EVERY_THEME_2026-11-21.md`),
continuation `customer-voice-theme-map-after-the-bill-shock-scheme-is-proven`. It applies the coding
scheme proven on the bill-shock page
(`how_households_respond_to_bill_shock_by_family_moment_and_payment_method_2026-11-21.md` §4–§5,
"the bill-shock page" below) across themes. The scheme is not restated or rebuilt here.

> **The skew, stated as the steer requires.** Coded accounts are self-selected and skewed to the
> angry and the delighted. They show **which reactions exist and how they chain**, never how often.
> Every count of accounts on this page is a count of *published stories found*, not a share of
> households. Every rate on this page comes from a representative or census source and is graded:
> **P** published from a census or weighted sample, **S** single or self-selected source, **A**
> analyst arithmetic on published figures.

## 0. What this increment is, and what it is not

**Done for this increment:** (1) the theme frequencies from the representative sources that a
supplier or the public can read, (2) every coded account found so far set against the theme list,
(3) each theme placed on the world and supplier sides as they stand in the tree today, and (4) each
theme given its route: a world item, a supplier item or a graded insight. The work items are handed
on through `background.seat_continuation` (§5).

**Not done:** a deep account sample per theme. Published case-study sources are thin outside
billing (§2.3), and Trustpilot's terms forbid automated access. The hand sample of review sites and
forums is the advisor's offered pass after the 23 Nov reset; this page names what that pass must
find (§6). Price-shock accounts are still not coded, as the bill-shock page §5.3 item 5 requires.

**The Ombudsman's own dispute taxonomy has six categories:** Billing, Customer Service, Payments,
Smart Meters, Standard Meters, Other. Shares of each large supplier's (over 1m accounts) accepted
disputes, Q1 2025:

| Category | Range across the 8 large suppliers |
|---|---|
| Billing | 48%–65% |
| Smart Meters | 5%–21% |
| Customer Service | 7%–14% |
| Other | 7%–14% |
| Standard Meters | 2%–5% |
| Payments | 1%–5% |

**Signposting:** suppliers reported correctly referring 55% of cases (Q4 2025) and 53% of domestic
cases (Q2 2026) after 8 weeks or deadlock. The bill-shock page cites 48% for 2025 from the annual
data; the quarterly figures are higher, and both are carried.

**What the taxonomy says by itself.** Switching, home moves, final bills, supplier failure,
vulnerability and green or EV tariffs are **not** Ombudsman categories. They fall in "Other" or are
recoded into Billing (a final bill dispute is a billing dispute). So no census source gives those
themes a frequency, and the theme map must not read one off "Other". The 2016–2025 history is also
missing here: the oldest pack linked is Q1 2023.

**Selection, which applies even to a census.** A case reaches the Ombudsman only after 8 weeks or a
deadlock letter; its caseload is ~2–2.5% of complaints (bill-shock page §2.1, A). The category
shares are therefore shares of *unresolved* disputes, which weight hard-to-fix problems (smart
meters, billing errors) above quick ones (a call-wait complaint resolved on the day).

### 1.2 Ofgem: complaints reported by suppliers, and the satisfaction survey — P

From `ofgem.gov.uk/news-and-insight/data/data-portal/customer-service-data`, read 21 Nov:

- **Complaints recorded by suppliers, Q2 2026: 936 per 100,000 accounts** in the quarter (large
  919, medium 1,529, small 161); down 11% on Q2 2025. No category breakdown is published by Ofgem;
  each supplier publishes its own "top 5" reasons on its website (not yet read: §6, V2).
  Annualised, roughly 3.7 complaints per 100 accounts a year (A: four times the quarter, assuming
  no seasonality, which is not established).
- **Energy Consumer Satisfaction Survey** (BMG for Ofgem and Citizens Advice, twice a year,
  Jul–Aug 2026 wave): overall service satisfaction 78%; easy to contact 67%, difficult 8%;
  switching-process satisfaction 87% among switchers; billing-accuracy satisfaction 81% and
  bill-understanding 84% among credit-meter customers. Sample sizes are not on the page.
- **The survey's contact reasons are not on the web page.** Citizens Advice cites the January 2026
  findings report for two claims that the report itself does not state (corrected 23 Nov from the
  advisor's Pack C read of the report, `docs/staging/advisor/done/DIRECTOR_EVIDENCE_CUSTOMER_VOICE_PRICE_SHOCKS_REFUNDS_MOVES_SOLR_EV_AND_THE_REPORTS_2026-11-23.md`;
  cite the report by figure number, its page numbers differ between reads):
  - **"Smart meter problems are the most common complaint" is not a stated finding.** In the Jan
    2026 complaint reasons (Fig. 39, n=96, below the report's own caution base of 100) smart meter
    problem is **31%, second to net billing at 70%**. Among all contact reasons (Fig. 25, n=1,473)
    the most common is **meter reading, 16%**; smart meter query is 14%. Not carried as P.
  - **The "22% told it could not be resolved or passed on" is an inference, graded S.** Fig. 49
    gives smart-problem outcomes of resolved by supplier 49%, **passed to another organisation
    10%**, still being looked into 9%; the 22% appears to be the 10% plus about 12% "couldn't be
    resolved at the moment", whose label was not cleanly mapped. Use 10% (P) for "passed on".

### 1.3 Citizens Advice smart-meter polling — P (weighted), S (single wave)

*Fully Operational* (Citizens Advice, Aug 2026; Savanta, 4,000+ GB adults Mar–Apr 2026, weighted
to GB adults, 200 digitally disadvantaged by phone):

- **1 in 4 people give a manual meter reading at least monthly**, and among households *with* a
  smart meter the share giving at least monthly manual reads ranges **17%–29% across the six largest
  suppliers** (15%–27% excluding London). Supplier-reported "operational" smart meters range
  88%–95% across the same six.
- Households with installs in the last year report more issues and more manual reads.
- **19% of people without a smart meter say they refused one** because of others' bad experiences
  or media reports (17% in 2023).
- Prepay and credit smart customers are equally happy with the meter (65% against 67%), but 44% of
  prepay say it did not meet their expectations of new ways to top up.

### 1.4 Citizens Advice supplier rating (the public's league table) — P

Weights: complaints to Citizens Advice, the Extra Help Unit, the Ombudsman and Advice Direct
Scotland per 10,000 customers (35%); ease of contact (30%: call wait 18%, email in 2 working days
6%, messaging 6%); billing and metering (25%: bills on a read within 6 months smart or 12 months
non-smart 15%, smart meters working 10%); commitments (10%). It is the one published ranking a
household can see, so it is an **observable** a real supplier knows it is graded on.

### 3.1 Themes the evidence adds

- **The accountability gap with a third party** (DCC, the other supplier, the industry record).
  O2, O7 and the 10% "passed to another organisation" (§1.2). It crosses smart meters and
  switching. Kept as the `supplier_handling` codes `blamed_third_party` and
  `passed_between_suppliers`, not a new theme, because it is a way of handling, and the scheme
  already reads handling against outcome.
- **Statutory compensation not paid automatically** (O8; Citizens Advice's case for a smart-meter
  guaranteed standard). It is the supplier lane's question "does the supplier pay the GSOP it owes
  without being asked", and it joins the switching supplier item.

### 3.2 Themes the evidence drops or merges

- **Vulnerability** is kept as a circumstance, not a theme page (§3, row 11).
- **Final bills and refunds** merge with billing in every census source; kept as a theme only for
  the forum sample to test.

### 3.3 The cross-theme pattern, now on 18 accounts

The bill-shock page found that every escalation followed a handling failure. On the wider sample:
of the 9 escalated accounts, **8 state the handling, and all 8 record a handling failure** (O1, O3,
O4, O5, O6, C6, C7, O8); C5 does not state it. Grade **S**: existence and order only, from sources
that publish disputes. It is a hypothesis for the supplier lane's handling × outcome cross, not a
rate. No account in either sample records a switch after bad handling (bill-shock page G3 stands).

## 4. Validation targets this page adds (named before any run)

A miss is a finding about the world's behaviour, never a dial.

- **V-T1, complaint mix at a desk.** In any world year from 2023, billing is the largest category of
  the world's escalated disputes at every large desk, and smart meters second or third. Ordinal,
  from §1.1. A numeric band is not set: the only census shares are 2025 onward, outside the
  2016–2025 record for most years, and the Ombudsman recodes categories over time.
- **V-T2, the smart-meter spread.** Where the world runs several desks with different
  fault-resolution handling, their smart-meter dispute shares differ; a world in which every desk's
  share is equal cannot reproduce the 4× spread. Ordinal.
