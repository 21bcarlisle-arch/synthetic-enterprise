## 1. What was asked

For the register-error design (absent, stale, wrong): (1) direction and size of drift between a certificate and the real dwelling as the certificate ages, and the effect of RdSAP/SAP version changes; (2) EPC coverage by tenure and by time since transaction, and whether the uncovered stock is the long-tenure, older, worse-fabric stock; (3) whether SERL or NEED publish EPC-predicted vs metered consumption in a form that could judge a simulated result independently of RdSAP.

## 3. What is GAP

- **GAP-1: size and sign of drift by certificate age.** No source found that stratifies certificate-vs-reality (rating, fabric flags or metered use) by years since lodgement. Few et al. 2023 compare latest EPC with 2021 metered use by band only; the paper includes EPC lodgement year only as a variable in a correlation table (value 0.24 against another variable, not a gap by age). Neither the MHCLG release nor the EHS gives it.
- **GAP-2: net direction of rating change between successive certificates for one dwelling.** Crawley et al. (2019) hold exactly this data (1.6M repeats) but the full text was not retrievable (publisher blocked access; UCL repository link did not resolve). Their abstract gives noise only. This is the single most useful document to read next. Selection warning for anyone using it: dwellings re-lodged are those that sold or let again, or were assessed after an upgrade scheme, so they are not a random sample of the stock.
- **GAP-3: size of the RdSAP version step.** Direction is documented (RdSAP 9.94 in 2018 raised rated efficiency through new wall U-values). No published figure for the point shift per dwelling was read, and nothing on RdSAP 10 or SAP 10.x versus SAP 2012 for the same home. The Government's Home Energy Model consultation exists (https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/home-energy-model-replacement-for-the-standard-assessment-procedure-sap) but was not read.
- **GAP-4: EPC coverage by tenure.** Not published. MHCLG states coverage varies by tenure and lists "investigate data linking to provide data on tenure" as a future development. The EPC register's own tenure field is flagged as inconsistent for new dwellings. Only proxies exist (row 8): sale and rental lodgement counts per year, with no tenure denominator read.
- **GAP-5: coverage by time since last transaction, and share of uncovered homes that are long-tenure owner-occupied, older, worse fabric.** Not published. The mechanism is implied by the law (certificate only on construction, sale or let) and by the 79% ever, 52% valid pair: roughly 27 points of stock have had a certificate that has since expired, and 21 points never had one. Who those 21 points are is not stated.
- **GAP-6: age distribution of lodgements.** Not read as a distribution. Annual flow is 1.62M per year against a 10-year validity window, which is consistent with a roughly even spread, but the actual share of valid certificates lodged in each year was not tabulated. The open-data bulk file (needs a login) would give it; this agent did not use it.
- **GAP-7: SERL or NEED comparison by certificate age.** None found (see section 5).

## 4. Implication for the three register error channels (absent, stale, wrong)

Direction, as far as published evidence goes:

1. **Stale is improvement-dominated, so old certificates are pessimistic. Label: supported, magnitude GAP.** Stock fabric and rating improve in every tenure (rows 1-2), certificates are never refreshed after an upgrade (row 4), and Few et al. identify undocumented upgrades as a candidate for part of the gap. Two cautions. First, the physical fabric part of the rise is slow (loft +3 points, wall insulation +6 points of stock in a decade), so a pessimism of the size of the full 7-point SAP rise is an upper bound for a 10-year-old certificate, not an estimate, because part of that rise is boilers, glazing and the 2018 methodology step. Second, the average hides a mix: some homes improved and most did not.
2. **Wrong, as a methodology-version channel, shares the direction.** The documented step (RdSAP 9.94) raised ratings for the same home, so a pre-2018 certificate under-rates the home compared with today's method (direction established, size GAP). It is separable from improvement but has the same sign, and also grows with certificate age (older certificates are more likely pre-2018). A design should not treat the two as independent noise.
3. **Wrong, as measurement noise, is symmetric by construction but not uniform.** Crawley: error is large at low ratings (about 8 points at 35) and small at high ones (2.4 at 85). Worse-rated homes are noisier. Regression to the mean follows: a very low certificate is more likely to be rated too low than too high, which again makes the worst-banded certificates look pessimistic on average. This is inference from the published error structure, not a measured sign.
4. **Wrong, as the model-vs-meter gap (row 10), is not a certificate-age effect.** It is a property of the method applied at lodgement: the modelled figure exceeds metered use, more so for poor bands. It should not be confused with staleness. Judging a simulated register on that axis needs the band-level gap, not an age-level one.
5. **Absent is transaction-driven, and the register is therefore biased to homes that transact.** Half of existing-home lodgements follow a marketed sale and over a third follow a letting (row 8). A home that has not sold or let since 2008 and was never part of an assessment scheme has no certificate; the stock with no certificate is therefore by construction the longest-held. That it is also disproportionately owner-occupied, older and worse-fabric is likely but not published (GAP-4, GAP-5). Note a counter-pressure: scheme lodgements (green deal, ECO) put some long-held, poor-fabric homes on the register, though that share is small in recent quarters.

**Do they share one cause?** Plausibly, with qualification. Time since last transaction fits absence directly (law), explains why a certificate is old at all (an old certificate means no transaction since), and gives the stale direction (more time, more unrecorded upgrades). It does not by itself explain version-change error (that is a calendar effect, tied to the lodgement date, which is the transaction date of the certificate, so it correlates with the same clock) or measurement noise (assessor noise has no time dependence, only a rating dependence). The supported single-clock claim is for absent plus stale plus version step; noise is a separate, rating-driven channel. Two further caveats on the shared-cause story: a long-held home that was upgraded privately is stale and pessimistic but may be fully correct on fabric today; and a long-held home that was never upgraded has an old certificate that is accurate on fabric, so "old" does not imply "wrong" for that home. Uncovered homes are the unobserved end of the same distribution, not a separate class.

## 5. Judging a simulated result: SERL and NEED

- **SERL (Few et al. 2023) can judge level and band slope, not age.** It gives modelled-minus-metered gap by band (row 10) for 1,374 gas-heated homes using each home's latest EPC. It is independent of the simulation's RdSAP generator in the sense that the metered side is smart-meter data. Limits: homes volunteered into SERL, gas-heated only, one comparison year (2021), no gap by certificate age.
- **NEED:** published statistics report metered consumption by banded characteristics including energy rating; no certificate-age cut was found. Its insulation flags are scheme installs only (see the 2026-11-11 note), so they cannot stand for fabric state.
- **Nearest fallback for age.** (a) English Housing Survey physical inspection: each surveyed dwelling gets a surveyor RdSAP assessment of the real current state, so EHS mean SAP over time (row 1) and its measure prevalence (row 2) give the stock-level improvement rate that an old certificate misses. It is independent of lodged certificates but uses RdSAP, so it cannot validate RdSAP-derived numbers, only the improvement trend and prevalence. (b) Crawley's repeat-certificate analysis (GAP-2) once read. (c) The bulk open data (login required) can give repeat-lodgement band changes by gap in years directly; that would be the first real measurement of GAP-1 and GAP-2 and is a data task, not a literature one.

## 6. Recommendations (research, not build)

1. Treat "old certificate is pessimistic" as a stated belief with a range, sourced to rows 1-4, not as an established size. Magnitude must carry a GAP until GAP-1 or GAP-2 is filled.
2. Anchor absent-share to 21% never certificated and 27% expired-uncovered (row 7, England, to March 2026), not the older "about 60%" line.
3. Do not model the version step and improvement drift as independent; both have the sign "old certificate under-rates the home".
4. Keep modelled-vs-metered gap (row 10) as its own channel, band-driven, not age-driven.
5. Highest-value next reads: Crawley et al. 2019 full text; repeat-lodgement change by years elapsed from the bulk open data; the EHS technical notes for the size of the 2018 U-value step.
