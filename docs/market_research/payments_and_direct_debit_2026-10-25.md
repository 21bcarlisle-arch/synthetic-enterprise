## 2. Setting and reviewing a fixed monthly direct debit

**SLC 27.15 before October 2022:** "Save where a clear and express Principal Term of the relevant Domestic Supply Contract provide otherwise, the licensee must take all reasonable steps to ensure that the fixed amount of the regular direct debit payment is based on the best and most current information available (or which reasonably ought to be available) to the licensee, including information as to the quantity of electricity/gas which the licensee reasonably estimates has been or will be supplied under the relevant Domestic Supply Contract."
https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2022-06/Strengthening_fixed_DD_rules_stat_con.pdf

**The 2022 modification.** Ofgem's decision letter of 26 August 2022 removed the Principal Term carve-out. It also says the "all reasonable steps" qualifier would go, making the duty absolute. The change took effect 56 days after the decision, on 21 October 2022 per the licence notice.
- Decision letter: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2022-08/Decision%20letter%20DD%20rules.pdf
- Licence notice: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2022-08/Decision%20Standard%20Licence%20ELECTRICITY.pdf

**Settled 25 Oct 2026 (third read, below).** Ofgem's consolidated electricity supply SLCs ("Consolidated to 1 August 2025") give 27.15 as: "The licensee must ensure that the fixed amount of the regular direct debit payment is based on the best and most current information available (or which reasonably ought to be available) to the licensee, including information as to the quantity of electricity which the licensee reasonably estimates has been or will be supplied under the relevant Domestic Supply Contract." Both the Principal Term carve-out and "all reasonable steps" are gone, so the duty is absolute. Ofgem notes the consolidated copy is "not [a] formal Public Register document"; the gas licence was not read.
https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2023-03/Electricity%20Supply%20Standard%20Consolidated%20Licence%20Conditions%20-%20Current.pdf

**Consultation responses.** Two respondents asked for quarterly or "on-demand" reviews, and two proposed a 12-month minimal-balance requirement. Ofgem did not prescribe a review frequency in the licence.
https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2022-08/Decision%20letter%20DD%20rules.pdf

**July 2022 review of 17 large suppliers:**
- Between February and April 2022, more than 7 million SVT customers saw a DD increase. The average increase was 62%.
- 8% of those (about 500,000 households) saw rises of more than 100%.
- Results: four suppliers had no significant issues, seven had minor weaknesses, and five had "moderate to severe" weaknesses.

https://ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/ofgem-requires-improvements-energy-suppliers-customer-direct-debits

- 12 suppliers were in formal engagement. Of 56 concerns raised, 53 (95%) were resolved.
- Almost 1 million DDs were in scope of the increase review. About 4,000 issues were confirmed and £117,580 in compensation was paid.
- Improvements secured:
  - More frequent adequacy assessments.
  - "controls that trigger an additional adequacy review when a Direct Debit rises beyond a set tolerance".
  - Surplus credit either refunded or factored into a lower DD.
  - Faster refunds.
  - Formal DD and refund policies where none existed.
- Ofgem's expectation: "Where a consumer is in a credit position, we expect suppliers to reduce the associated Direct Debit level."
- Ofgem noted that debit balances from estimated billing are often recovered over 12 months, and that a quarterly cap combined with 12-month forecasts calls for regular adequacy reviews.

https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/decision/direct-debit-market-compliance-review-progress-update

- From 20 September 2023, suppliers must hold cash of at least 20% of gross domestic credit balances net of unbilled consumption, or Ofgem can direct ringfencing.
- Domestic credit balances were about £7.1bn gross and about £4.1bn net (about £140 per customer) in Q1 2023.

## 3. Bacs direct debit mechanics

**The three-day cycle.** Day 0: submit between 07:00 and 22:30. Day 1: parties receive the message at 06:00. Day 2: the payer is debited and the merchant credited. Failure notices arrive through another cycle. In about 1% of cases they arrive on day 4, so a payment is not final until then.
https://gocardless.com/direct-debit/timings

**Advance notice.** The default is "10 working days", which can be reduced by agreement with the payer.
https://gocardless.com/direct-debit/timings

**AUDDIS (setting up the instruction).** Responses arrive about 3 working days after a Direct Debit Instruction (DDI) is submitted. Example codes:
- 5 = no account
- B = account closed
- F = invalid account type
- G = bank will not accept DDs on the account
- L = incorrect account details

https://www.gocardless.com/direct-debit/receiving-messages

**ADDACS (payer amendments and cancellations).**
- 0 = instruction cancelled, refer to payer
- 1 = cancelled by payer
- 2 = deceased
- 3 / C = account transferred
- B = account closed
- D = advance notice disputed
- E = instruction amended
- R = reinstated

https://www.gocardless.com/direct-debit/receiving-messages

**ARUDD (failed collections).**
- 0 = "Refer to payer", usually insufficient funds
- 1 = instruction cancelled
- 2 = payer deceased
- 3 = account transferred
- 4 = advance notice disputed
- 5 = no account
- 6 = no instruction
- 7 = amount differs
- 8 = amount not yet due
- 9 = presentation overdue (collected more than 3 working days after the notified date)
- A = service user differs
- B = account closed

https://www.gocardless.com/direct-debit/receiving-messages

**DDICA (indemnity claims).** Under the Direct Debit Guarantee, the payer's bank refunds immediately for payments taken "in error or without authority". The bank then raises a DDICA and recovers the amount from the collecting business. There is no strict time limit on payer claims. The business's window to challenge is quoted inconsistently, as both 9 and 14 working days.
https://www.accesspaysuite.com/blog/direct-debit-indemnity-claims/

**Bacs volumes, 2024.** There were 4,939,796k direct debits and 125,258k "unpaid debits". The ratio is about **2.5% of all DDs** across all sectors **[derived]**. The source does not link the unpaid figure to ARUDD.
https://www.wearepay.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Bacs_Annual-processing_stats_2024.pdf

Pay.UK reported a record 5 billion DD transactions in 2025.
https://www.wearepay.uk/direct-debit-hits-record-5-billion-transactions-in-2025-retaining-its-place-as-the-uks-go-to-for-recurring-payments/

**Energy DD failure rate.** BFY Group's analysis of ONS and Ofgem data puts the average failure rate at 2.2% in 2024, up 27% on 2023. This is a secondary source and its method is not published. **[unverified]**
https://www.bfygroup.co.uk/blog/why-is-energy-debt-still-going-up

## 5. Migration between payment methods

**Involuntary prepayment.** Ofgem reviewed more than 150,000 PPMs fitted under warrant or remotely switched between 1 January 2022 and 31 January 2023. Suppliers committed £18.6m (£5.6m in compensation, £13m in debt write-off) on 28 May 2025. Rules now require "at least 10 attempts to contact a customer" and a welfare visit before an involuntary installation.
https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/suppliers-commit-further-ps186million-customer-compensation-and-debt-write-following-ofgems-prepayment-meter-review

**Customer inertia.** "only 20% of customers on Standard Credit surveyed wanted to change payment method."
https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-05/Appendix-5-Decision-Impact-Assessment.pdf

**Awareness.** 43% of standard credit customers know it costs more than DD (Ofgem consumer research, cited by Fair By Design).
https://fairbydesign.com/blog-standard-credit-price-cap-2025/

**Why customers leave DD.** No primary dataset gives the reasons. The commonly cited drivers (bill shock from DD increases, mistrust over credit balances, and arrears leading to PPM) are inferred from the 2022 reviews above. **[unverified as quantified]**

## 6. Cost to serve and bad debt by payment method

**2018 payment method uplift (2017-18 terms, dual fuel), standard credit vs DD:**

| Cost element | Standard credit | Direct debit |
|---|---|---|
| Working capital | £21 | £0 |
| Bad debt | £37 | £9 |
| Admin / cost to serve | £27 | £7 |
| Total | £80 | £11 |

The final 2017-18 differential, after headroom, was £75. Ofgem's reasoning: direct debit payments "minimise the chance of non-payment". Standard credit customers "pay a higher proportion of their bills in arrears". Admin cost was £19.09 per account at the benchmark supplier.
https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2018/11/appendix_8_-_payment_method_uplift.pdf

**2025 debt-related cost decision, effective 1 July 2025:**
- Allowance per dual fuel customer per year: £57 DD, £160 standard credit, £19 PPM. The overall benchmark is £71.
- Rationale: to "maintain a premium that reflects the higher direct cost and risk of serving a Standard Credit customer".
- Debt costs peaked in summer 2023 at "well over twice the level seen in the previous year".

https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-05/Appendix-2-Decision-Debt-related-costs.pdf

**Cap levels, April–June 2024:** £1,690 DD, £1,796 standard credit, £1,643 PPM. PPM levelisation started on 1 April 2024: £10 added to DD and £49 taken off PPM.
https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-02/Default%20Tariff%20Cap%20Letter%20-%201%20April%202024%20.pdf

**Standard credit premium.** £135 a year from July 2025.
https://fairbydesign.com/blog-standard-credit-price-cap-2025/

**Average bills, 2025 (DESNZ).** Credit £2,015, DD £1,884, prepayment £1,817.

  probability `DD_SHARE_ELEC = 0.72` / `DD_SHARE_GAS = 0.75` (DESNZ QEP, end-March 2026) and calls everything

  rate is named gap 4 above, which is unpublished.

## Off direct debit and back on: what the supplier may do (seat, 26 Oct 2026, PB8 L2)

The page said nothing about re-mandating. Before PB8 L2 was built, the seat recorded these points.

**A mandate ends by one of two routes, and the supplier sees both.** (a) The supplier cancels it. British
Gas: "If your payment fails for a second time, we'll have to cancel your Direct Debit and send you a bill
instead" (https://www.britishgas.co.uk/help/struggling-to-pay/what-happens-if-i-dont-pay.html, read
2026-10-03). This is `DD_STOP_THRESHOLD_CONSECUTIVE_RETURNS` in `company/billing/dd_collections_desk.py`.
(b) The payer cancels at its own bank, and the supplier reads ADDACS code 1, "cancelled by payer" (§3),
from the working day after the bank inputs it (`dd_failure_basis_and_live_arrears_provision_rates.md`
§4a). In both cases the account then pays on receipt. That is standard credit, and StepChange warns it
is billed "at a higher rate" (https://www.brighouseecho.co.uk/news/people/dont-pay-uk-expert-warns-why-you-should-not-cancel-energy-bill-direct-debits-3804057).

**A new mandate needs the payer's instruction.** A Direct Debit Instruction is the payer's authority, lodged
through AUDDIS (§3). A supplier can invite a household to sign one. It cannot reinstate a mandate the
household cancelled, and it cannot reopen one it cancelled itself without a new instruction. So a
re-mandate has two authors. The supplier decides whether and when to ask. The household decides whether
to sign.

**What is published about the supplier's side.** Power NI asks for a missed DD payment "within 5 working
days to remain on Direct Debit" (https://powerni.co.uk/help/bills-payment/direct-debit-management/what-if-i-miss-a-direct-debit-payment/).
That is the step before a stop, not a re-mandate. No GB supplier publishes when it invites a stopped or
self-cancelled account back onto DD. **GAP.**

**What is published about the household's side.** No energy figure. No take-up rate is published for a
supplier's invitation back onto DD, and no flow from standard credit to DD is published (named gap 7
above). **GAP.** Two figures from elsewhere bound a belief (26 Oct, `gb_payment_method_migration_rates.md`,
"A take-up rate for an invitation back onto DD"):
- a randomised trial of a DD prompt to council tax payers not on DD (Medway, 2017): 5% signed within a
  month with the best insert, and about 3.5% with none;
- Ofgem's 2015 survey, read with DECC's 2015 payment shares: at most about 4% of standard credit
  payers a year moved off pay-on-receipt while staying with the same supplier.

**Which choices the design makes, and why.**
- *The desk's policy is its own, and a named simplification.* The desk invites an account back once it has
  paid `REMANDATE_OFFER_AFTER_PAID_BILLS` on-receipt bills in a row by their due date, and it reads only
  its own ledger to decide that. After a decline it waits the same count again. The count is the
  supplier's choice, not a fact about the world. Nothing published bounds it, and the code says so beside
  the constant.
- *The world's answer is a labelled range, and the lower edge ships.* `simulation/bacs_rails.py`
  `REMANDATE_ACCEPTANCE_PROBABILITY` is 0.035 per invitation, from an EVIDENCE RANGE of 0.035 to 0.05.
  The lower edge ships (F11) because two things are unmeasured, and both would make 0.05 an
  overstatement. One is whether a second ask after a decline pulls as well as a first. The other is how
  far a household that recently left DD differs from a long-standing non-DD payer, and nothing gives
  the direction for both routes. On the 40-household spell book, over 8 seeds, 1 of 28 stopped
  households is back on DD by the end of the book's year at 0.035, and 3 at 0.05. That is within the
  ≤4% a year that Ofgem's survey bounds. *(Until 26 Oct this was `None`. The run put no invitation to
  a household, so every stop was terminal and manual payers were overstated.)*
- *ADDACS 1 reaches the desk.* When a household cancels its mandate (DD4b or own-reasons, both shipped at
  0), the month's collection comes back "instruction cancelled" (DD4b's own calendar). The desk then
  reads the ADDACS 1 record dated the next working day after that return, and cancels the mandate from
  that date. The supplier-stop route and the household route now leave the same trace: the mandate is
  cancelled in the register, the account pays on receipt in the money side, and the seam answers
  `standard_credit` from the notice date.
- *A stop is a spell, not the end (seat, 26 Oct 2026, built before any rate).* The rails hold each
  household's spells off DD: the last bill presented by DD before the stop, the notice date, and, if
  the household signs again, the last on-receipt bill before the re-mandate and the AUDDIS confirmation
  date. The rails feed the desk its own on-receipt ledger: whether each bill was paid by its due date, on
  the outcome the money side draws for that bill. The money side (`arrears_engine.paying_method`, and the
  billing ledger through it) and the seam (`StopNoticeBoard`) read the same spells. So a rate, once
  sourced, moves the register, the P&L and the renewal price together. A household that cancelled at its
  own bank and later signs again is collected again: its earlier cancellation is spent. When the spells
  landed the rate was None, so nobody was invited and no shipped number moved. The rate set on 26 Oct
  relabels a few bills as DD again. Supplying a rate in a
  test relabels bills and moves no payment outcome, because a resi outcome is drawn the same way on
  either channel. That stops holding once DD and on-receipt payment behaviour differ, so a rate and any
  such difference each need their own pre-registration.

## A DD is never late (seat, 28 Oct 2026)

**What the rules require.** A Bacs collection is taken on its processing day or returned through
ARUDD, within a day or so of it (§3; `dd_failure_basis_and_live_arrears_provision_rates.md` §2). A
returned item may be re-presented within one month of the original processing day (ARUDD 0). So a DD
has two outcomes on its date: collected, or returned. Money that arrives weeks later after a return is
a re-presentation or a payment by another route. It is not the DD succeeding late.

**What the world did.** `arrears_engine.payment_outcome` gave every domestic credit bill one draw:
returned at the sourced first-presentation rate, otherwise on time at `_ON_TIME_PROB`, otherwise
paid 3 to 90 days late by stress tier. A DD took the late leg too. At real inputs (400 bills a tier,
due 2016-03-20) it was 25 late at LOW, 190 at MODERATE (14 to 45 days) and 337 at HIGH (30 to 90).
The 1a02c46116 book shows it as 181 accounts with DDs collected 31 to 45 days late and no return
(`INV_PAYMENT_RECORD_RECONCILES_TO_COLLECTIONS_DUE`).

**The choice, and why.** A DD that does not fail is collected on its due date. The late draw is still
made, so no later draw moves, and it is kept for pay-on-receipt methods. The DD is not turned into a
return. The sourced first-presentation return rate already counts every DD not collected on its
date. Adding the late leg as returns would put HIGH-stress DD failure near 90%, which no source
supports. **Direction of error:** this favours the company, because stressed DD payers now pay on
time. Their only shortfall is the tier's return rate, whose MODERATE and HIGH relativities are a
named simplification (`arrears_engine._DD_TIER_RATIO_TO_LOW`). If those tiers understate stressed
returns, this change carries that understatement into timing as well. Whether a paid bill is paid
does not change on either channel. Only when it is paid does, so the re-mandate note above, "a resi
outcome is drawn the same way on either channel", now holds for paid-or-not and not for the date.

**Not changed, then changed (29 Oct 2026).** This page first left a mover's final bill with the exit
model's own lateness (`final_bill_outcome.PAID_LATE`), even on a DD leg, and named it as the first place
to look. The fresh 209c3350ab book confirmed it. Every one of the row's 38 "missed DD without a return"
bills (35 accounts, graded on origin's one definition of a missed payment) is a mover's:
- 17 are the final bill, a DD "collected" 3 to 77 days late with no return;
- 21 are the bill before it. The triad asked the exit model with that bill's due date, which falls in the
  move month, where the model reads the period end. So a mid-tenure bill was drawn as an exit.

**The choice.** A paid final bill on a DD leg is collected by the existing mandate on its due date. That
is the stayer's rule above, and "collecting the final bill by the existing direct debit" is published
move-out practice (`home_moves.md`, "Debt prevention at move-out"). Whether the bill is paid, part paid or
unpaid still comes from the exit model, so no pound moves, only the date. The bill before the final is
drawn like any other. **Direction of error:** this favours the company, because a gone household's paid
DD now arrives on time. **GAP:** no published share of movers who cancel the mandate before the final
collection, which would turn some of these into returns followed by payment by another route.
