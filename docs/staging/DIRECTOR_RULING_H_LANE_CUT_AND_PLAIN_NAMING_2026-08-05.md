## Ruling 2 — Plain-words naming law (map-wide, lanes and atoms)

The director's words: letters and atom numbers do not communicate the architecture; he cannot judge whether groupings are logical without words or pictures. Therefore:

- **Every lane and every atom carries a plain-English title** (a few words, subject not mechanism) **and a one-line gain statement** — what the company or machine gains when this is done. Mechanism vocabulary (draw, worktree, mutation, marker, hook) lives in description fields, never titles.
- **Ids become machine keys.** Grammar-enforced, registry-checked at mint, and never shown on any human surface — chat, site, reports, NTFYs all render titles.
- **The map's site view renders titles**, lanes as named shelves, level as the visual dial; ids are debug-hover at most. The didactic rule already governing site graphs applies to the map picture.
- **Acceptance test (falsifiable):** the director, reading a lane's atom titles cold on his phone, can spot a misfiled atom. A misfiling invisible in titles is a naming failure. This test is run at each epoch close alongside consolidation.
- Field mechanics (whether `name` is repurposed or a `title` field added) are CC's choice; the law is the readable surface, not the schema.
