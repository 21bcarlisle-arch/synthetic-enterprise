---

## 1. The households are generated, not selected

The sample is **modelled houses, each placed in a coherent weather cell, with a coherent set of people living in it.** Real data calibrates and checks it; real data is not the thing we select from.

So NEED — and any comparable survey — is **evidence, not population.** Fit the joint from it: how fabric, floor area, era, type and retrofit state co-occur, and how they drive consumption. Then generate households from that joint, place each at a location that puts it in a real weather cell, and give it people consistent with the dwelling. The acceptance test is unchanged, and it is the calibration: the weighted set must reproduce the observed distribution.

**Two reasons this matters, and both were live defects when it was decided.**

Selecting real rows made the source's limits our limits. NEED covers England and Wales, so **no Scottish dwelling could be chosen at all** — and Scotland is the coldest 8% of the book, where weather sensitivity is largest. Generation reaches it; selection never could.

And coverage was capped at the combinations a 46,000-row sample happened to contain. A joint can produce a combination that is real and rare without a survey having sampled it.

**The risk that comes with generation is implausibility** — a 1900 solid-wall dwelling with modern airtightness, five people in a studio, a heat pump in an uninsulated house. Draw from the fitted joint rather than independently, and make plausibility a control rather than an assumption.

---

## 2. Effort follows where value is lost, not where volume is

Standard practice samples in proportion, so effort follows volume. **That is the wrong instinct here and it is inverted deliberately.**

Common combinations are already well served. A real supplier's segment model handles them, and the customer gets a reasonable outcome. **The value is in the uncommon combinations** — the ones an averaging model rounds to the nearest segment and gets wrong, where customers are disappointed or undervalued. That is what hyper-personalisation is for.

**So the sample is densest where the population is thinnest.** The choosing deliberately over-weights the tail; the solved weights restore the mass when aggregating. Both halves are required and neither is a compromise.

---

## 3. One case stands for many, and that is a chosen property

With chosen cases and solved weights, a single household may carry the mass of tens of thousands. This is correct for aggregate economics and for demonstrating per-case reasoning, and it is the answer to simulating a supplier of millions without simulating millions.

**It has a stated limit.** Because near-duplicates are rejected by construction, the world contains no two households that are *similar but not identical*. So the design can show the company beating average on a **distinct** household. It cannot yet show it separating two households that look alike and behave differently — which is the harder and more commercially real problem.

**Two things must remain reversible, and nothing may be built that forecloses them:**

- A weighted case can later be **inflated into a small population** with within-group variation, once the physics is settled.
- The count on high-volume cases can be **raised** where a use case makes that worth doing.

Neither is built now. Both must stay possible.

---

## 4. What the world is ultimately for

The aim is a simulation carrying **near-full billing, commercial and practical complexity** — what a real supplier must actually handle. Estimated and actual reads, catch-ups and rebills, three payment methods with different mechanics, moves and deemed contracts, arrears and collections, tariff changes and renewals, the levy stack, settlement on three clocks.

**A household is therefore more than usage and shape.** It needs a meter type and read pattern, a payment method, a tariff with its dates, a move history and a credit position — the things that make a bill right or wrong.

**And a sample sized against the demand vector may not be sized against billing complexity.** Two households with identical consumption, one on quarterly estimates and one half-hourly, one on direct debit and one in arrears, are entirely different to bill and to serve. Whether the demand sample spans that is an open measurement, not an assumption — and **the demand sample must not be built in a way that forecloses adding those axes.**

---

## 5. The premise this all rests on

The SIM holds the hidden why. The company sees only what a real supplier could see. Value comes from inferring the gap, never from access.

The synthetic book exists so that a supplier with the economics of millions of customers can be simulated at a size that can actually be run — and so that the customers who do not fit the average, who in a real book get suboptimal treatment precisely because they are uncommon, are **over-represented rather than rounded away.**
