## 1. A GAP LIST IS NOT A PAGE (class rule)

A page whose body is an enumeration of what it does not cover is **machine exhaust presented as content** — the same class as the simplifications register occupying the lay-reader slot on `/simplified`. 

**Rule:** named gaps live in the **residuals rung**, never in the body. The body contains explanation of what we *do* understand. **If there is not enough understanding to fill a body, ship a STUB** — identity, place in the graph, and "not yet written" — which the brief already declares a legitimate state. A stub is honest. A page that is mostly its own to-do list is a stub in costume, and it is worse than a stub because it claims depth it does not have.

## 2. THE SCOPE BRIEF MUST BE INDEPENDENT — transcription is not scoping

The updated page's gap list is substantially the director's off-the-cuff list. That is **assembly-first displaced one level**: no longer starting from what we hold, but starting from what the director last said. **Neither is the domain.**

A scope brief asks: *what must a complete treatment of this topic contain, for a reader who needs to understand it?* — answered from the domain, from practitioner expectation, from the structure of the market itself. The director's list and our existing material are both **inputs to check the brief against**, never the brief. Evidence that this was done properly: the brief contains material neither the director nor the repo supplied.

## 3. THE NATURAL JOINT — gas and electricity are SEPARATE topics

Director's ruling: **gas wholesale and electricity wholesale are treated separately.** They differ in all three of the things that define a topic:

- **Products:** NBP/TTF, within-day through annual contracts, storage products — versus baseload and peak blocks, EFA periods, seasons, day-ahead auctions.
- **Mechanisms:** storage cycles, LNG cargo arbitrage, pipeline and terminal flows — versus merit-order stacking and the marginal plant.
- **Drivers:** global LNG competition, European storage levels, weather-driven heat demand — versus gas price, carbon price, wind, demand, interconnector flows.

They are **coupled, not merged**: gas price drives electricity price for most GB hours. **Coupling is an EDGE (`drives`), never a merger.** A page that treats them as one topic cannot describe either well — which is what happened.

**The general test, applied to every topic:** *do these things share products, mechanisms and drivers?* Shared → one topic. Different → separate topics with a typed edge. **Apply it across the domain; if you judge the joint sits elsewhere for some pair, argue it with the test.**
