## 1. THE DEFAULT: proceed at risk, record for reversal

**Reversibility is achieved by RECORDING, not by ASKING.** For anything that can be undone:

1. **Make the recommendation.**
2. **Proceed.**
3. **Record what was done, why, and precisely how to reverse it** — one line, in the ledger.
4. **Flag it** in the batched [ACT] as *done, reversible, here is the undo*.

The director reverses if he disagrees. That costs one act **occasionally**, versus one act **per item** under the current design. Blocking is the expensive option and it has been the default.

## 2. THE TEST — apply it to every reserved clause we hold

**Can this be undone by a single act, with no external consequence in the meantime?**

- **YES → proceed at risk.** Recommendation, action, recorded undo, flagged. This is the default and needs no permission.
- **NO → reserved.** Genuine one-way doors: real customers, real money, real external communication; safety-control and authorization-trust changes; anything that destroys evidence or cannot be un-published.

**Curriculum values (R13) remain reserved** — not because they are irreversible, but because they are the science: they define what the experiment measures, and an agent choosing its own difficulty is the R12 breach the rule exists for. That reservation is narrow and stays.

**Everything else is a candidate for release.** Level moves are reversible — a level can be demoted, and R16's real requirement is *ledger-backed, not self-certified*, which recording satisfies. A BUILD_OPEN on an already-ratified decision is not a decision; it is plumbing, and should release on ruling consumption.
