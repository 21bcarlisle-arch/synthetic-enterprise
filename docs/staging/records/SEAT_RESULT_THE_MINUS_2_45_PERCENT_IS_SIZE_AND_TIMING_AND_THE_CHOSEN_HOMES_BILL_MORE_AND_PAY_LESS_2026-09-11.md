### The answer to the question that was asked

**On gross margin the −2.45% is SIZE AND TIMING.** The blind arm of the same shape lands at

### The finding I did not expect, and it is the more useful one

**The chosen homes BILL MORE and PAY LESS.** Held at identical count, identical year mix and
identical customer-year spend, choosing for difference buys:

* **+0.56% more revenue and +0.56% more gross margin** than a blind book of the same shape — the
  chooser is *not* picking commercially worse homes, it is picking bigger-billing ones;
* **+31.92% more bad debt** (£10,916.13 → £14,400.86) on that same shape;
* and the bad debt wins: **net margin −3.02%**, net after cost to serve −4.17%.

**This settles P6's flagged unknown.** P6 recorded +23.34% bad debt on a 6.37% smaller book as
*"the one I would not have predicted and did not"*, and guessed the cause was the chooser pulling
in tails. **It is now measured and the guess was right**: the blind arm of the same size has bad
debt *below* the 91-account cull (£10,916.13 against £11,675.76), so none of the +23.34% is the
book being smaller. **All of it, and more, is which homes.**

### Which leg dominates depends on which line you read, and that matters

On **gross margin** — P6's headline line — size and timing is the whole story and composition is a
small positive. On **net margin** the two legs are **almost exactly equal and both negative**
(−2.82% and −2.93%). A reader told "the total follows the size" from the gross-margin line would
carry that to the net-margin line and be wrong by half. **Per customer-year, with the budget held
across all three arms: gross £897.94 / £871.10 / £875.96; net £346.25 / £336.48 / £326.33** — the
orderings are not the same.

**Where my reasoning went wrong, beside the claim.** I argued that because all three arms spend the
same customer-years, revenue must track customer-years and any difference must be composition. The
error is that a customer-year is not a unit of revenue: the arms spend the same customer-years **in
different years**, and 2016–2025 contains 2022. ARM C holds B's year mix, which is weighted earlier
and carries 5 accounts through the crisis year against the cull's 3 — so moving from A to C moves
the book's exposure across the price history, and that is worth −2.99% on its own. **The quantity I
should have held constant was not customer-years but customer-years-by-year, and holding it is what
the next arm has to do.**

## What this arm cannot say, stated plainly

* **The A → C leg is TWO variables, not one.** ARM C holds *B's* year mix, so A → C moves count
  (91→83) and timing together. **The C → B composition leg is clean at one variable; the other leg
  is not.** Splitting it needs a fourth arm: 83 accounts at **A's** year mix. That is the next item
  and it is the one that would let anyone say how much of −2.99% is fewer and how much is earlier.
* **One seed.** The runs are deterministic at a fixed seed, so these differences are exact for this
  world and carry **no bound across seeds**. The two large legs (−2.99% size-and-timing, +31.92%
  bad debt) have signs worth stating; **the +0.54% gross-margin composition leg is small enough
  that one seed cannot bound it and it should not be quoted as a directional result.**
* **Not publishable pounds.** All arms ran `SIM_FAST_MODE=1`, identically, so the *differences* are
  clean and the absolute figures are not comparable to `docs/reports/run_output_latest.json`.
  Nothing here belongs on a page as a headline.
* **A labelling artefact.** ARM C reaches the book through `choose_settled_sample`, so its run
  records `settlement_selection: chosen_weighted` though nothing about it saw a home. It is a blind
  arm wearing the chooser's label; no figure depends on it.
* **Downstream drift is effect, not confound.** The arms' funnels diverge slightly after the
  settlement decision (2019: 70 vs 72) because a different book changes the company's later plans.
  The intervention is at the settlement decision, which is verified identical in shape; everything
  after it is the treatment.
