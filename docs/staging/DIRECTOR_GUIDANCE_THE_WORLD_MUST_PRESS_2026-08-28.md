**The mission does not change.** Poesys is an autonomous UK energy supplier run inside a faithful simulation of the real market, cutting carbon through personalisation, measured in £ per tonne of CO₂e saved, with "could a real supplier know this?" as the test on every line. Nothing below alters that sentence. What follows says the simulation is not yet faithful in three specific ways, and that this bounds what any result about the company can mean.

## Part 1 — Three corrections to THE MODEL ON A PAGE

Filed under the canon's own binding reading rule: where the page and the built reality disagree, surface the disagreement as a finding. These are claim-status defects, not rewrites. Confirm, correct, or reject each on the evidence; the F-numbering is the worker's.

### C1 — "coupled hidden traits" is unearned

The page says the drawn population carries *"coupled hidden traits."* The traits are drawn and coverage-tested — `price_sensitivity`, `green_stance`, `channel_pref` — and are read by no live module. Every response function is universal: two households identical in company-observable terms and opposite in trait behave identically. Mutual information is zero, not small.

The world therefore **records** heterogeneity rather than **expressing** it. Until a trait changes a behaviour, "coupled" overstates what exists. Correct the claim or make it true; do not leave the page asserting it.

Related and equally unearned: the segmentation curriculum declares axes discoverable through channels that do not exist. A trait documented as discoverable, with no channel carrying it, makes a competent company look incompetent forever — it will correctly try, and correctly fail, indefinitely.

### C2 — the market does not compete

The page says *"acquisition & churn (market-coupled swell proven)."* Reading the real switching record is genuine coupling to history and should stand. What does not exist is a competitor: no module models a rival supplier; the comparison price is the published SVT series read by date from a quarterly table; and market position is `PRICE_DIFFERENTIAL_PCT`, a single run-level constant for the whole run.

So nothing in the world responds to what the company does. Nobody undercuts it, nobody defends, nobody targets its book. State this as plainly as the page already states *"moneyness trigger absent — known"* — naming the gap in the same breath as the capability is the page's own convention and it works.

**Why this is the most consequential of the three.** Every measurement of the company's decisions is currently taken against an opponent that cannot move. Over-pricing carries no competitive consequence, so an expected-value maximiser correctly discovers that charging more is close to free — that was never a defect in the arm, it was the arm reading the world accurately. "Beating the flat baseline" compares two internal policies in a market that could not react to either. That is a valid internal comparison and it is not evidence about a supplier's performance.

### C3 — SURVIVE means "not guaranteed to live", and its mechanisms are named

The page's score is Survive, Earn, Abate, and says the company *"can be wrong, and it can die."* The director's clarification, which should replace the mortality framing: **the company does not have to die. It has to not be guaranteed to live.** Survival must be genuinely at risk on a path the world can actually produce.

Three mechanisms carry that risk in reality, and they are the ones to make press:

- **Hedging** — a supplier hedged short into a price spike faces collateral calls before revenue arrives. The 2021–22 record is already in the world; that is exactly when real suppliers failed.
- **Competition** — the slow death. Lose the best accounts to someone cheaper, retain the expensive ones, and the book decays into unprofitability with no single dramatic event. Blocked entirely by C2.
- **Debt** — arrears rise in precisely the periods when wholesale costs peak, so the cash squeeze and the margin squeeze arrive together.

What these share is that they are **cash and timing** problems, not profit problems. The company has three clocks and a treasury and no mechanism that converts a bad quarter into a liquidity constraint. That is why it looks comfortable, and why EARN has been the only leg the world can press on.

"Upgrading the SIM to make it work harder" means specifically: the world imposing collateral calls, competitive loss and non-payment on timings the company does not control — and the company having to survive them rather than merely record them.

## Part 2 — The problem inventory

Problems with their evidence. **Not a priority order** — see Part 3.

**P1 — No competitor; the market replays rather than competes.** Evidence in C2.

**P2 — Household variation exists and cannot be observed.** Traits drawn, read by nothing (C1). Separately: where variation is real, no channel carries it — `price_sensitivity` reaches the world only where it sets the retain outcome, so the company cannot infer it through anything it sees. Variation without a channel is indistinguishable from no variation; these are one problem, not two.

**P3 — The published heterogeneity is in the wrong place.** Ofgem/BMG conjoint (n=3,235, Jul 2025): price weight varies only 1.26× between the most and least price-focused subgroups, and spend-versus-switching correlation is essentially zero. The large published spread is in **satisfaction with the current supplier (1.46×)** and a **17% minority prioritising exit fees**. Note also that a subgroup range is a between-group statistic and says little about within-group variance; elasticity is typically close to orthogonal to observables, which is why couponing, time-limited offers and price walks work at all. Draw genuine within-household variance, not a subgroup mean shift.

**P4 — Engagement and elasticity are not separated.** Whether a household enters a choice process at all is a different thing from how far price moves it once it does. A large share of GB households have never switched at any price — an engagement fact, not an elasticity one — and a disengaged household can be highly price-sensitive once a bill shock makes it look, which is exactly why bill shocks are predictive. Modelling one on the other's shape builds a world where never-switchers are also price-insensitive, and the company will learn a relationship that does not exist.

**P5 — £ or % is unresolved and systematically distorting.** If the switching decision keys on percentage saving, every household is equally worth chasing per point of margin. If it keys on £, a large home is far cheaper to win because the same percentage buys more visible saving, and customer value scales with consumption in a way a percentage world misses entirely. GB comparison sites and the cap headline both present annual £. Establish what our world uses, what the evidence says, and reconcile.

**P6 — Decisions are still lookup tables.** Acquisition budget takes no customer id; dunning keys on segment and days overdue while `capacity_to_pay` and the breathing-space register go unread; retention is a fixed offer and fixed tiers. A rich, competitive world cannot be exploited through a segment table.

**P7 — Survival has no cash mechanism.** Hedging, competition and debt do not yet threaten liquidity (C3).

**P8 — No systematic link from knowledge to discovery to atoms to epochs.** Published evidence we already held sat unused while a 3.75× spread was invented. A July design document sat unread until a director question surfaced it. Discovery ran eight passes deep on atoms that could never build. Knowledge pages describe mechanisms with no link to the atoms implementing them, so page and code can disagree indefinitely and nothing notices. Today discovery produces documents and the map produces work, and the two meet only when a human connects them in conversation. This is the velocity multiplier: fixing it makes everything else cheaper.

**P9 — Book depth bounds every comparison.** Only 37 accounts carry five or more renewals, so nothing can compound. Book age moves the P&L, which makes it curriculum and therefore the director's — flagged, not delegated.

**P10 — Operational floor.** The publish gate is slower than the cadence it gates and its own analysis says raising the ceiling cannot fix it. Uncommitted work accumulates in three places at once — the tree, undeclared worktrees, and thirteen branches holding unadopted authoring work. The divergence alarm counts its own output. Repeating alarms still do not escalate into work. Two tests failing at HEAD, against a ten-day-old baseline so "newly failing" means "not in an old list". Roughly 117 harness atoms have never been read for whether they should exist. And the model-tier pilot — routing mechanical work below Opus — was ordered and there is no evidence it ran; that is the difference between a machine that can be left running and one that cannot.

## Part 3 — How to take this, and the one real trap

**This is not a sequence and must not be executed as a list.** The delivery seat sequences it. That is precisely the job that seat holds, and it has measurements neither the director nor the advisor has.

**The one genuine cul-de-sac:** P1 invalidates measurements taken before it. Anything calibrated against a market that cannot respond will need recalibrating once it can. So work whose *result* depends on the world pressing back should not be tuned against the current world — and anything built now against P2–P6 should be built so that P1 changes its answers, not its structure. Say plainly where that tension bites rather than discovering it later.

**Deliberately out of scope for now, by director decision:** the carbon score, and products beyond price (efficiency advice, tariff fit, solar, heat pumps, time-shifting). Both are real and both are downstream — measuring an unbuilt capability is premature, and the products need a world that can reward them. They return once the basics hold.

**Use the machinery that already exists.** Mint these as atoms in their proper lanes, attach them to the epochs that gate them so they accrete rather than scatter, and let the ceiling and the draw do their work. Nothing here needs a new process.

**One addition to the delivery seat's subjects:** the canon. Today's three corrections were found because the director and advisor happened to read the page — no mechanism ever checks it, so the mission sits structurally outside the machine's field of view. Every orientation should ask not only what is drifting in the work, but what the page claims that the code no longer supports. That check would have caught C1 and C2 weeks ago, and it is the machine's job rather than the director's.
