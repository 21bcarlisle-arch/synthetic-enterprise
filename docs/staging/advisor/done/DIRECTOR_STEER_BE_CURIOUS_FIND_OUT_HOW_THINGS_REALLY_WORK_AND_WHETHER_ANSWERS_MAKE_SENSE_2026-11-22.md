# [DIRECTOR-STEER] Be curious: find out how things really work, and whether answers make sense

**Type:** STEER, through the advisor channel. The director's words, from chat, Sun 22 Nov 07:51 GMT, after the call-time question reached him with Citizens Advice unsearched:

- "In general we have good knowledge at the start of things, but we lack a little curiosity and desire to learn. To figure out more deeply how stuff works and if answers make sense."

## What he is pointing at

The machine is good at gathering a first layer of knowledge. It is weaker at three things:

- **going deeper** into how a thing actually works;
- **asking whether an answer is plausible** before using or publishing it;
- **noticing when something does not add up.**

Recent cases from origin and this chat, gathered by the advisor:

- **Call times:** a question raised to him before the most obvious public source was searched.
- **The value test's "+£5k":** published for weeks as a book total. No one turned it into about £5 per household per year and asked whether that meant anything. He asked.
- **72% of homes drawn as POOR insulation,** against a true figure of about 15%. It stood until a check caught it.
- **Bill shock:** he had to supply the structure himself (two families, three SVT moments, payment method). The first framing asked him for one number.
- **The value test's renewal belief with an AUC of 0.52** produced a margin gain the site led with.

The common thread: **the nonsense was found by him, or late, rather than by the machine asking "does this make sense, and why does it work like that?"**

## The intent

Curiosity becomes part of how the machine works. It is not a new rulebook. **The design is the machine's. Build it into what exists**, as the 14 Nov one-work-system steer asks, and add as little as possible. Possible shapes, for it to weigh, not prescriptions:

1. **"Does this make sense?" is part of using a number, not a later check.** Every figure that feeds the world, the supplier or the site is checked against an independent second view before use:
   - a second source;
   - a per-household or per-unit restatement (CLAUDE.md's "before dividing two numbers, say out loud what each counts");
   - an order-of-magnitude estimate;
   - a physical or accounting identity.

   The knowledge page records which check it passed. "Print the numbers at real inputs" already exists for formulas. Extend it to every number used.
2. **Ask how it works, not only what the number is.** When a knowledge page states a rate, it should also say **what causes it and what would move it**: the mechanism. A page that gives only a number is unfinished. Mechanism is what lets the world respond to events, as in his bill-shock structure.
3. **Surprise is work.** A result that surprises, an outlier, or a figure that disagrees with intuition or the trade becomes an item in the one work system. It is ranked, not explained away in a commit message.
4. **A practitioner's eye on knowledge, not only on outputs.** Cold reads currently read books and pages. Point the same practice at knowledge pages: would someone who has run a GB supplier find this complete, and would they believe it?
5. **Measure it in the learning loop.** Count **"nonsense found by the director or the advisor before the machine found it"**: implausible numbers, missed obvious sources, questions he could answer from public data. It should fall. It is the honest measure of curiosity.

## The advisor's own share

The advisor holds itself to the same standard. It sense-checks before relaying, and says when a relayed figure does not make sense. It missed the £5k framing until he asked.

## Ask
