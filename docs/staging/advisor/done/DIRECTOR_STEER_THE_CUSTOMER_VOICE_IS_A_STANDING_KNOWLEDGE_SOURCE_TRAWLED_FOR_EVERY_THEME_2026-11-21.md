# [DIRECTOR-STEER] The customer's own voice is a standing knowledge source, trawled for every theme, not only bill shock

**Type:** STEER, through the advisor channel. The director's words, from chat, Sat 21 Nov 07:23 GMT, after the advisor listed the sources for the bill-shock page (reviews, Ombudsman, Citizens Advice, MoneySavingExpert forums, Ofgem surveys):

- "These are great data sources. Shouldn't these be trawled for other themes and insights ?"

## The intent

Yes. What GB customers say about their energy supplier is the closest thing this project has to the real world's own voice. It should be a **standing source in the knowledge base**, mined for **every theme**, not opened once for one page. It speaks directly to his 30 Oct steer that pain points drive the loop: knowledge, then the world reacts, then the supplier tests, then nuance.

## What is asked (the machine owns the design)

1. **One customer-voice source, coded once and read many ways.** Sample and code accounts:
   - from review sites (Trustpilot and comparison sites, within their terms; sample and code, never bulk-scrape);
   - Energy Ombudsman decisions and annual reports;
   - Citizens Advice supplier ratings, complaint data and case studies;
   - MoneySavingExpert energy forums;
   - Ofgem consumer surveys.

   Use one coding scheme across themes, so a single coded account can feed several pages. The fields:
   - **theme**;
   - **trigger event**;
   - **payment method and meter type**;
   - **customer circumstance**, where stated (vulnerability, prepay, debt, home move);
   - **first response**;
   - **supplier handling**;
   - **outcome** (stayed, switched, escalated);
   - **time and effort spent**;
   - **emotional tone**.

   The time, effort and tone fields speak directly to M3's needs: time, certainty, control.
2. **A theme map first.** Before going deep anywhere, find which themes recur and how often. That frequency is biased toward the angry and the delighted; take rates from representative sources. Expect at least:
   - billing errors and catch-up bills;
   - estimated bills and meter reads;
   - DD changes;
   - switching and onboarding;
   - smart meter installs and failures;
   - debt, collections and prepayment;
   - home moves;
   - final bills and refunds;
   - supplier failures and the 2021-22 SoLR transfers;
   - customer service access;
   - vulnerability;
   - green and EV tariffs.

   Let the evidence add more and drop some.
3. **Each theme becomes work, through the one work system.**
   - Where the world does not yet produce the pain, it is a **world-lane item**.
   - Where the supplier has no handling for it, it is a **supplier-lane item**.
   - Where it reveals a pattern or a wrong belief, it is an **insight**, with a trust grade.
   - Every theme page cites the coded accounts behind it.
4. **Keep the source alive, but bounded.** Refresh on the standing monthly reading cadence (the 5 Nov reading-log ruling), not continuously. Each refresh asks one question: what is new or changed since last time?
5. **Know its limits, on the page.** It is self-selected and skewed to extremes. It shows **which reactions exist and how they chain**, not how often. Rates come from representative sources. State that on every page that uses it.
6. **No personal data (W11).** Keep codes and short paraphrased examples only, with no names, account details or identifying text.
7. **The bill-shock page is the first worked theme.** That is the 21 Nov 07:15 steer and its 07:20 addendum. Build the coding scheme there, so it is proven on one theme before it is applied to all.

Place it in Monday's weekly review: first in the knowledge share, with its cost. The advisor offers to run a first broad trawl after the Mon 23 Nov reset, producing a draft theme map with example accounts, staged as evidence for the machine to check and deepen.
