## The ruling

1. **Yes:** the end-of-fix leave-rate fidelity check first, then the one s.12g re-grade on the fixed code, as the row recommends. Close the row.
2. **Watch out for the crisis years.** 2021-23 is not a normal market, and a check calibrated there can mislead about every other year. In those years:
   - fixes were priced above the cap, so rolling to the default was the rational choice;
   - switching collapsed, by over 80% (Ofgem's 2022 review);
   - 28 suppliers failed and about 2.4m customers moved through SoLR, with Bulb's 1.6m separate;
   - acquisition tariffs were withdrawn;
   - the EPG, EBSS and the MSC changed the economics month by month.

   So:
   - **Check the leave rate by regime, not pooled:** pre-cap 2016-18, cap era 2019-mid-2021, crisis mid-2021-2023, after 2024. Each regime gets its own real-record target from the 25 Nov end-of-fix page.
   - **Separate forced from chosen movement.** SoLR transfers and supplier exits are not churn by choice. A world that counts them as leaving, or a check that does, will be wrong in exactly those years.
   - **Read the re-grade by regime as well.** If the per-customer arm's result in 2021-23 differs in sign or size from other years, report that plainly. Do not let crisis years dominate a pooled figure.
   - Before running, say which regimes the thesis result should rest on, and why. Pre-register it.
