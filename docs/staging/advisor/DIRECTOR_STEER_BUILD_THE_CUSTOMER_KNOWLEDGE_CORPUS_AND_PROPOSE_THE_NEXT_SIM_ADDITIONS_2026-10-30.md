The advisor's synthesis for the director:
1. **The framework holds.** Cost, cash flow, time, carbon and peace of mind appear as separate drivers in large GB studies.
2. **Two moderators are missing:** trust and capability. They are the axes that separate Ofgem's own six attitudinal segments.
3. **Two needs split in two:**
   - control splits into control over spend and control over devices;
   - time splits into clock time and bandwidth, and bandwidth is depleted by financial scarcity.
4. **Most needs sit on two axes.**
   - **Precarity** drives cash flow, spend control, worry and the comfort floor together. Its signals are tenure, low income, deprivation, single parenthood, disability and prepayment.
   - **Affluence** drives carbon concern and the value of time.
5. **Price sensitivity is at least two traits, and neither follows wealth in a line.**
   - **Consumption elasticity** is need × headroom, capped by the comfort floor.
   - **Switching or shopping propensity** is need × capability × trust × hassle, and it is anti-correlated with need.
   - This points the same way as the existing design item that separates engagement from elasticity. Check how far that has got before building.
6. **Life stage often explains more than income does.**
   - Worry peaks at 35–54.
   - Risk aversion is U-shaped by age.
   - Cash buffers rise steeply with age.
   - Single parents are the most exposed group on almost every need.
   - Life events shift the whole needs mix at once (canon M10: act at moments of action).

## The ruling: parsimony in the drawn world

The director rules this for now:
- **Correlate only strongly correlated variables in the population draw.** Draw a small number of latent factors from the attributes that strongly predict them: precarity, affluence and life stage are the candidates. Leave the rest independent.
- **Do not build a full joint distribution of every need with every attribute.** That needs sample sizes that grow with every permutation.
- **Keep product and service decisions separate.** Attitude to bills and the decision to buy an EV are different choices. The customer chooses each one, conditioned on the few latent factors that matter to it. There is no need to unbundle them into one joint preference model.
- **Add a correlation only when evidence shows it is strong and that it changes a supplier result.** Record each addition as a dated design decision.
