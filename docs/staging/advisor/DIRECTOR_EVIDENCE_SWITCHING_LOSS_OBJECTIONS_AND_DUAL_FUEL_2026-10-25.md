## 1. The switching process before and after CSS

**Before CSS (to 17 July 2022)**
- Ofgem's "as-is" map sets out the steps. The customer accepts a quote, and a "Cooling off period (14 days)" follows. The gaining supplier registers the MPxN on MPAS (electricity, "an overnight batch process") or UK Link (gas). Then the "Losing supplier has circa 1 week to register an objection; if it does, the switch is cancelled." After that come agent appointments, the change-of-supplier read, and the final and opening bills. "Current average switching time are between 15 and 16 calendar days but can be longer." Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2018/02/as_is_switching_arrangements.pdf
- Objection windows were "up to 7 working days for gas and 5 for electricity". Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2017/09/policy_update_objections.pdf
- The three-week rule (2014) required suppliers to "take all reasonable steps" to switch within three weeks of the Relevant Date. That date is the end of cooling-off, or earlier if the customer agrees to start sooner. The 14-day cooling-off period came from the Consumer Contracts Regulations in June 2014. Before the rule, more than 20% of domestic electricity transfers and more than 80% of domestic gas transfers took longer than three weeks. Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/ofgem-publications/87151/statutoryconsultationenforcethreeweekswitchingandpreventerroneoustransfers.pdf
- In November 2016 Ofgem said most switches took about three weeks, down from about five weeks three years earlier. Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/switching-45-year-high-more-people-shop-around-better-deal

**After CSS (from 18 July 2022)**
- The CSS went live on 18 July 2022, "the same day that the Central Switching Service went live" alongside REC v3.0. Sources: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2022-10/Switching%20SCR%20closure%20letter.pdf and https://xoserve.com/change/investment-change/switching-programme-css
- Licence (SLC 14A.1, as drafted): the gaining supplier must complete a transfer "within five Working Days" of the Relevant Date. If the customer has not asked to start early, the clock runs from the end of the Cooling-Off Period. A contract made after 5pm on a working day counts from the next day. Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2021-12/Electricity_Supply_SLC_Notice%20-%20SCR%20StatCon%20-%20FOR%20PUBLICATION.pdf
- REC Registration Services Schedule (a June 2019 draft, so check it against the live REC):
  - Domestic supply start must be "at least one complete Working Day (starting at midnight) after the day on which the Switch Request is submitted". Non-domestic is two working days. A switch can be dated up to 28 days ahead.
  - The domestic objection window closes "at 17.00 hours on the 1st Working Day after the day on which the Gaining Supplier submitted the Switch Request". The losing supplier receives an "Invitation to Intervene".
  - A registration becomes "Secured Active" at 17:00 on the day before the start date. The gaining supplier can withdraw, and the losing supplier can annul, only before that point.
  - Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2019/06/rec_registration_services_schedule.pdf
- Ofgem planned a standard 5-working-day switch just after go-live, "to allow more time to catch Erroneous Switches". Source: policy_update_objections.pdf, above.
- Compensation: from 1 May 2020, the gaining supplier paid £30 if a switch took more than 15 working days. From 1 April 2024 that threshold became 5 working days, "shorten this time from 15 to 5 working days". Sources: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/publications/new-customer-compensation-payments-further-improve-switching and https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/decision/supplier-guaranteed-standard-performance-switch-speed-decision
- Cooling-off cancellation is the gaining supplier's job. "It is for the Gaining Supplier to withdraw the switch". Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-02/R0287-and-R0287A-Authority-Decision.pdf

## 2. Objections

- **Grounds.** Domestic objections are allowed under SLC 14 for debt (14.4(a): "Outstanding Charges are due"), for erroneous transfers, at the customer's request, and where related meter points have not all transferred. For non-domestic customers, contractual objections apply instead. Sources: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/system/files/docs/2016/07/impact_assessment_on_review_of_domestic_objections.pdf and the as-is map above.
- **Debt rule.** The supplier must first have asked for payment, and "the debt must have remained outstanding for more than 28 days". Under the Debt Assignment Protocol, prepayment customers with debt under £500 can switch if the new supplier agrees. The threshold rose from £200 to £500, and about 90% of the debt moves across. Source: the 2016 impact assessment above.
- **Rates before CSS:**
  - Annual objection rates were 6% for electricity and 5% for gas (March 2016). Debt objections were over 90% of all objections.
  - Debt objections numbered 374,463 in 2013, 285,697 in 2014 and 284,323 in January to September 2015.
  - About 70% of indebted transfers went ahead without objection.
  - Source: the 2016 impact assessment above.
  - Separately, "only around 5%" of switches faced an objection, and microbusiness gas was "in excess of 20%". Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2016/06/agenda_item_5-objections_summary_paper.pdf
- Ofgem kept debt blocking. It found it "unlikely to be in the interests of domestic consumers to remove the transfer blocking conditions". Source: the 2016 impact assessment above.
- **Under CSS,** an accepted objection on one part of a linked group (OFAF, "one fail all fail") cancels "all other Pending or Confirmed Registrations within the OFAF Group". Source: the REC schedule above.

## 3. The losing supplier's view: loss notices and saves

- **Notification.** The losing supplier receives Pending and "Invitation to Intervene" messages from CSS. Its only regulated responses are an objection (on the grounds above) or an annulment. Source: the REC schedule above.
- **Annulment cannot be used to save a customer.** REC change R0287 was approved on 20 February 2026 and implemented on 27 February 2026. It limits annulment to cases where "the consumer has not entered into a contract with the Gaining Supplier". Ofgem rejected R0287A, which would have allowed annulment where the customer wants to stay. The code manager's stated aim was to "prevent retention-based misuse". If a customer changes their mind, the gaining supplier withdraws the switch. Source: the R0287 decision above.
- **Ban on acquisition-only tariffs (SLC 22B).** It has been in force since 14 April 2022 and "ensures that any discounted deals available to a supplier's new customers are also available to existing ones". Sources: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-07/Decision_on_removing_the_BAT_July_2024.pdf and https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-11/Decision_Future_of_the_Ban_on_Acquisition_only_Tariffs_after_March_2025.pdf
  - The ban was extended to 31 March 2026 (November 2024 decision) and then to 31 March 2027 (November 2025 decision).
  - Ofgem is "minded to extend the BAT, and retain its associated market-wide derogation, until 31 March 2028". A decision is due in November 2026.
  - Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-08/Renewal-of-Ban-on-Acquisition-only-Tariffs-after-March-2027.pdf
- **Does a retention offer have to match acquisition tariffs?** The rule works the other way round. Acquisition deals must also be open to existing customers. A market-wide derogation lets suppliers offer "bespoke 'retention-only' deals" to customers "reaching the end of a fixed term contract". Ofgem reports "negligible real-world evidence" of its pricing effect. Source: the November 2024 decision above.
- **Market Stabilisation Charge** (14 April 2022 to 31 March 2024). The gaining supplier compensated the losing supplier for part of its hedging costs. Source: the July 2024 BAT decision above.
- **Gap:** I found no domestic rule that names "save" or "win-back" contact after a loss notification. The only evidence of win-back tactics is non-domestic and undated (Energywatch on British Gas Business). Source: https://www.energyadviceline.org.uk/news/latest-news/winback. My recollection, **[unverified]**, is that general marketing and Standards of Conduct rules would govern such contact.

## 4. Erroneous transfers (ETs)

- **Rates:**
  - About 1% of transfers (2014). Source: the three-week consultation above.
  - About 0.5% of switches among the six large suppliers, roughly 25,000 a year, and about 1% for non-domestic (2016). Source: https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/2016/08/erroneous_transfers-policy_issue_paper.pdf
- **Causes.** "The majority of ETs occur due to incorrect switching data or consumer errors", most often an address and MPxN mismatch. Only "approximately 85%" of matching data was thought correct. Other causes are mis-selling, fraud and cancellations that were not actioned. Source: the 2016 ET paper above.
- **Old process (ET Customer Charter, MRA MAP 10 / SPAA Schedule 10).** Written confirmation within 5 working days, and confirmation of the return "within 20 working days of their initial contact". The original supplier bills the customer. Source: the 2016 ET paper above.
- **Compensation.** From 1 May 2020 there has been an automatic £30 for an ET. Under the first tranche of standards, 27% of payments were for mistaken switches. Source: the 2020 compensation press release above.
- **Dual fuel.** Ofgem notes ETs "that may be caused by inconsistencies between electricity and gas data". Under CSS, dual-fuel switches are linked one-fail-all-fail. Sources: the 2016 ET paper and the 2017 objections policy update above.
- **After CSS.** In 2025–26 the Switching Address Quality Plan cut unmatched addresses by 700,000, but no ET count was given. Source: https://retailenergycode.co.uk/recco-at-five-five-years-of-building-a-better-retail-energy-market/

**Collapse and recovery**
- ElectraLink (electricity, voluntary changes of supplier, including non-domestic): 6.34m at the 2019 peak, 3.21m in 2024 (+38%), with "Overall switching in 2024 was at the same levels seen 10 years ago." Switching "peaks in April and October". Source: https://www.electralink.co.uk/2025/01/switching-rises-38-in-2024/
- Energy UK (electricity, excluding SoLR and internal): 2025 monthly totals sum to about 3.28m **[derived]**, of which about 2.87m domestic. Source: https://www.energy-uk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Energy-UK-Switching-Report-December-2025.pdf

**Dual fuel.** In 2016, about 70% of electricity customers and 80% of gas customers held dual-fuel accounts. Source: Ofgem November 2016 press release above. Gas transfers ran at about 0.79 of electricity transfers in 2025 **[derived]**. That ratio is a proxy, not a dual-fuel share.

**Internal switching.** 17% of customers "said they had switched with the same supplier in the last 6 months". More than three times as many customers switched internally as externally, up from about twice as many in November 2022 to January 2024. Fixed-term share was 34% in June 2026, against a low of about 9% in July 2023. Source: the August 2026 BAT consultation above.

## 6. Supplier of Last Resort, 2021–22

- 28 suppliers failed after June 2021. "Nearly 2.4 million customers transferred through the SOLR process". Bulb's 1.6 million customers went into special administration instead. The estimated cost was about £2.7bn, or about £94 per customer. Source: https://www.nao.org.uk/press-release/the-energy-supplier-market/
- SoLR transfers are excluded from QEP, Energy UK and ElectraLink voluntary counts. ElectraLink recorded 8.35m involuntary changes of supplier in 2023 and 7.45m in 2024, including acquisitions and SoLR migrations. Source: ElectraLink above.
