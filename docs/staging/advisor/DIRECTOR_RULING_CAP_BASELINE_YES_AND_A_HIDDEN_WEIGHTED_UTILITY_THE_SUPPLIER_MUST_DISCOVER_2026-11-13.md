# [DIRECTOR-RULING] The cap baseline: yes. One number: yes, as a weighted utility hidden in the world that the supplier must discover

**Type:** RULING, through the advisor channel. It answers `for_the_director` row `value-test-baseline-and-axis-weighting` (raised 13 Nov). The director's words, from chat, Fri 13 Nov 18:59 GMT:

- "Yes. No. I think a weighted utility score is a good idea and a way to capture enterprise customer value creation. NB this needs to be hidden in the SIM and underlying. The supplier must discover it ?"

The advisor had put the row's two choices to him with the machine's recommendation: the cap baseline, and the vector with no weighting. It added one suggestion of its own: call a win when an arm is better on at least one need and not worse on any.

## The ruling

1. **The cap baseline: YES.** As the row proposed: the standard-variable pair's flat arm charges the cap, the efficient-supplier allowance arm (rule 4) stays the primary baseline for renewals, and the £2/MWh bridge runs once.
2. **No weighting: NO.** He wants a **weighted utility score**: one number per household for the value created, so enterprise customer value creation can be captured and compared. Two conditions are his and are not open:
   - **It is hidden in the world.** Each household carries its own weights across the needs (money as cost and as cash flow, time, carbon, certainty, control) as part of its hidden life. It is ground truth, under W7's "truth stays an instrument". The world uses it to decide how the household responds to offers, and the scorer uses it to grade arms. **It never crosses the seam.**
   - **The supplier must discover it.** The company sees only what a real supplier sees: choices, switching, payment behaviour, contacts, survey and review feedback. It has to infer what each household values from that. How well it infers is itself a measure of the method (M4), beside the belief's skill.

## What this changes, and what it does not

- **M3 needs amending.** M3 says each need is "measured in its own unit and never converted to pounds". The vector stays: each need is still measured and published in its own unit. What is added is a world-side utility that weighs the needs **per household, by that household's own hidden weights**. It is not a single exchange rate chosen by the company or by the scorer. Draft the M3 wording that says this, and land it as a marked change to his canon citing these words (`Director-doc: his-words`). The advisor reads the draft before it lands. If the machine thinks a pound unit is still wrong for the utility, say so with a proposal.
- **Comfort and health stay a floor, not a weight.** No utility gain justifies a breach.
- **The value test headline becomes the per-household-year utility gain** of each arm against its baseline, with the vector beside it. The advisor's "better on one, worse on none" rule becomes a secondary read: it shows when a utility gain also holds on every axis.
- **M2 still holds.** Value created is the household's utility gain, and the supplier's share is its margin. A gain that comes only out of the household's bill is still transfer, and the utility score must show it as such.
