# [DIRECTOR-STEER] Knowledge and insight are different layers, and every claim carries a trust assessment (2026-11-04)

**Type:** STEER, through the advisor channel. These are the director's words from chat on Wed 4 Nov:

- 08:41 GMT: "I saw a knowledge article that seemed to be internal insight not external knowledge. I like it but we really need to distinguish between the 2. Insight might not be right. It's useful and an important layer to build. But it's different to external knowledge. Maybe all knowledge and insight needs a degree of trust/truth assessment?"

## The distinction

- **Knowledge** is about the outside world. It covers what external sources state, and what we derive directly from external data by a named method. Examples are Ofgem's cap rules, an EST measure figure, and a cross-tab we computed from Ofgem's own CIM tables. Under the 30 Oct data standard, each figure already carries a grade: D (derived by us from primary data), P (primary publication), S (secondary summary) or A (assumption).
- **Insight** is ours. It covers interpretation, synthesis, hypotheses and conclusions, whether we reach them by reading the knowledge or by running our own simulation. Examples are "age outweighs income", "precarity and affluence are two axes", "price sensitivity is two traits", and "reputation is a stock". Insight is valuable, and it is a layer to build on purpose. But it may be wrong, and it has to say so.

## Ask (you own the design; these are prompts)

1. **Label every page and every claim as Knowledge or Insight.** On the site, mark the difference at a glance. A page that mixes the two marks each section. Insight pages may live in their own section of the knowledge base, for example "What we think (insight)" next to "What is known".
2. **Give every claim a trust assessment, both layers.**
   - **Knowledge:** the D/P/S/A grade, plus whether it is verified against the primary source, and its date or vintage.
   - **Insight:** what it rests on (which knowledge, and which runs), a status (*hypothesis*, *tested*, *held*, *contested* or *refuted*), what would falsify it, and the last date it was checked. An insight drawn from our own simulation is labelled as such.
3. **No circularity.** An insight derived from our own simulation must never become a world parameter, or feed a calibration target. Otherwise the world ends up confirming itself. World parameters rest on knowledge graded D or P only, per the 30 Oct data standard. Insight may guide what to test or build, never what the world is calibrated to.
4. **Audit what is published now.** Classify every Knowledge page, and every claim in the new customer-needs domain, as knowledge or insight, with its trust assessment. Find the page the director saw, and pages like it. List anything currently presented as knowledge that is really insight, and fix the label. Report the counts.
5. **Review insight.** Every insight carries a review date. The cold reader, or an independent reader, challenges insights as well as figures. A refuted insight stays visible, marked refuted, with what refuted it. It is not deleted.

**Sequencing.** The labelling, the trust fields and the audit are drafting work. They can run now, beside the correctness stretch, in the same lane as the corpus work. The no-circularity rule binds immediately.
