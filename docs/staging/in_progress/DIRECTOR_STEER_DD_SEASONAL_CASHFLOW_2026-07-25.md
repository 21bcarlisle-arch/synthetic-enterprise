# [DIRECTOR-STEER] — Per-customer DD cash flow: the seasonal credit cycle, and why growth timing is a solvency mechanism (2026-07-25)

**Type:** [STEER] via advisor bridge. Director-authored domain content. Mechanism yours to design; the requirements below are the wall. FRAME first — establish what the billing organ already does before proposing anything.

## The mechanism the director has named

UK domestic direct debit is conventionally a **level monthly payment** — estimated annual cost divided by twelve — against consumption that is heavily seasonal. The consequence is a cash cycle that every real supplier lives inside and that the SIM must reproduce:

- A customer acquired in **spring or summer** pays level DD while consuming little. Their balance builds **credit** through summer.
- That credit is **the customer's money, held by the supplier** — cash on the supplier's balance sheet, and simultaneously a liability.
- Through **winter** the customer consumes far more than they pay, drawing the credit back down; a supplier that spent it must fund the winter energy from elsewhere.

**Therefore growth timing is a solvency mechanism, not a commercial preference.** A supplier acquiring aggressively in spring/summer sees cash pour in and can mistake it for the business working. Continued growth means new customers' summer credit funds prior customers' winter draw-down — **the structure is a Ponzi in all but name, and it holds only while growth continues.** When growth stalls, or wholesale spikes so the winter energy costs more than the credit collected, it fails. This is a documented cause of UK supplier failure and the reason customer-credit-balance protections exist at all — the SIM cannot claim to reproduce 2021–22 without it.

## Requirements

**1. Every customer carries a realistic individual cash flow.** Not a book-level aggregate. Specifically:
- **Staggered payment anniversaries** — start dates distributed across the year, never a common January reset. The whole phenomenon is invisible if every customer cycles together.
- **A balance trajectory** that builds and draws down with the seasonal shape of that household's actual consumption, against their level payment.
- **A starting balance that may be positive or negative** at acquisition — new customers do not all begin at zero. (Change-of-tenancy debt physics is already a registered DISCOVER atom; it feeds this and must not be duplicated.)
- **New customers' cash follows this same pattern** — so the aggregate cash effect of a growth cohort depends on *when* it was acquired.

**2. Cash held is not profit, and the site must never conflate them.** Customer credit balances are a **liability** that happens to be denominated in cash the supplier is holding. A supplier can look cash-rich and be insolvent — that is the tell this whole design exists to surface. Add the customer-credit-balance position alongside the three clocks (billed / settled / banked); do not let a treasury figure stand alone without it.

**3. The annual DD review is a first-class event**, not a silent recalculation: it is where a supplier's estimate meets reality, where large increases become complaints and churn, and where a bad forecast becomes a customer-experience failure. Model it as an event with consequences.

## Registered, not built — variable/seasonal DD, and the norms tension

The director's aside, recorded as a design principle worth its own name: **a seasonal DD (higher in winter, lower in summer) would track true consumption better, avoid large credit balances, and remove the annual-review shock — yet it is not the market norm and customers do not expect it.** This is the general tension the personalisation thesis must live with: **what genuinely serves the customer is not always what the market has normalised, and a supplier that ignores expectations loses the customer before the benefit lands.** Register as a candidate personalisation product for the growth/product session — the gap between true need and market norm is itself a modellable phenomenon and arguably where the product opportunity sits. Do not build a variable-DD product ahead of that session.
