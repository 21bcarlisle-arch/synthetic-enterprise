## The finding

The departure-headline defect was: a number correctly measured over one population, compared to a
published rate stated over another, and the verdict published as if the two were the same quantity.
It was found by hand, on one page, after it had been live for seven days — and the finding that
caught it called it **the fourth instance of the class in a fortnight**. The other three were
*average unit rate*, *net margin* and the journey's end having two homes; `CLAUDE.md` names the
shape directly ("Before dividing two numbers, say out loud what each one counts").

**No instrument asks this question anywhere.** Every control that exists checks that a figure is
arithmetically right, that it carries its clock, that its bound predates it, that the page agrees
with the producer. None asks whether the numerator's population is the denominator's, or whether a
comparison's two sides count the same thing. That is why four instances in a fortnight were each
found by a person reading a page.

## Why a census and not another hand pass

A hand pass finds the fifth instance and misses the sixth. The subjects are enumerable: every
constant in the regulation commons that states a rate, every call site that compares a measured
level to one, and every published field whose name ends in a rate or a share. The question to ask
of each is the same two-part one — *what does the numerator count, what does the denominator count*
— and the answer is either declared beside the figure or it is a finding.

## What done looks like

Not a register of rules. The smallest mechanism that can fail: a list of the comparison sites,
measured rather than typed, each one carrying either a declaration that the two populations match
or a named reason it cannot be established. The departure block's own `denominator` field is the
shape that works — a declaration sitting beside the number it describes, which a reader cannot skip
and a producer cannot quietly drop.
