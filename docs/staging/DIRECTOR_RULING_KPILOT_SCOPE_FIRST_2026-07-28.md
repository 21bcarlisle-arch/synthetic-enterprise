## 1. THE METHOD FAULT — and the ruling that replaces it

The brief stated that ~70% of the material already existed and that the thesis was a page ~80% **assembled and derived**. That framing determined the outcome: **assembling from existing material can only reproduce existing material, gaps included, because the material does not know what it is missing.**

**RULING — scope-first, permanently, for every knowledge page:**

1. **Write the scope brief FIRST, independently of what we hold.** What must a complete treatment of this topic contain, for a reader who needs to understand it? Sections, questions answered, phenomena that must appear.
2. **Then assemble against it.** What we have fills the brief; it does not define it.
3. **The DELTA between brief and assembly is the primary finding** — the named gaps in our own understanding, published as such. That delta is worth more than the page.
4. **Blind where possible.** This is the board-spec pattern that already works: a specification written by someone who cannot see the build. Propose how to get a blind or blind-ish scope brief for topics — the board is the obvious instrument, and Spec 004 is a worked precedent.

**The ~80%-derivation thesis is WITHDRAWN as a design goal.** It was a cost claim wearing quality clothes. Measure derivation if useful; never let it shape scope. (R12.)

## 3. Named content gaps (the director's list — not exhaustive, the scope brief governs)

Missing and material: **the structure of wholesale prices** — the traded product set (baseload and peak, seasons, quarters, months, day-ahead, within-day) and why shape matters, which the director notes is *possibly the most important thing we use*; **gas as the global anchor** — NBP/TTF, LNG, and why gas sets the power price for most hours; **weather** and its route into both demand and generation; **interconnectors** and imports; **CfDs** and renewable support, and what they do to the merit order and to what consumers actually pay; **negative prices** — when, why, how often; **seasonality** across the year.

The page tried to be a hub and a full treatment simultaneously and achieved neither. **Propose the decomposition**: which topic is the hub, which are children, where this page's content belongs, and which children are stubs versus full pages. **Your call — you can see the material and the graph; the advisor cannot.** If you judge a different cut better than hub-and-children, argue it.
