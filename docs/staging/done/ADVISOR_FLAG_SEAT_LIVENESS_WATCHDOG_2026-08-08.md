# [ADVISOR-FLAG] — The seat needs a pulse monitor that outlives it (2026-08-08, corrected)

**Type:** [REQUIREMENT — problem, not mechanism]. Director-prompted. **This version supersedes the first: its evidence section claimed a "second silent seat death, provably dead-at-login by 20:06 UTC" — that claim was FALSE**, built on the advisor misreading a tmux clock as a login timestamp; the director's firsthand account (one login, ~14:00 UTC) overrules it and is the record.

**The true motivating instance:** from 19:33 UTC, both observable channels (ntfy, origin pushes) went quiet for 90+ minutes while drawn work remained. Whether the seat was mid-build or dead was UNDECIDABLE from every remote surface the project has — director and advisor together could not classify the silence without the director physically at the machine. That indistinguishability is the defect, and it is real regardless of what the seat was actually doing tonight. (The auth-lapse class is separately real — the morning wake required a human login after the dark fortnight — and its cure, a long-lived token, is on the director's list; it pairs with, not replaces, this flag.)

**The requirement:** a daemon-side monitor, outside every seat invocation, that alarms the phone channel when no tick-completion evidence (pushed commit, completion marker — the mechanism's choice) has appeared for N minutes while the scheduler believes work remains. It must distinguish "idle because the plate is empty" from "silent while work is drawn," and it must fire regardless of WHY the seat is silent — auth, crash-loop, wedged clone, dead scheduler, or a long build exceeding its expected envelope (in which case the alarm is a progress-report request, not a death notice).
