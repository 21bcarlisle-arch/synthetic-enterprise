## The order

**1. Knowledge, now and wide.** Debt and collections; unbilled energy and revenue assurance; home moves; next best action; communications, sentiment and NPS; upsell and cross-sell; EV, solar and batteries as products. Knowledge lives in its own folder and does not collide with the simulation, so it runs in parallel with everything below rather than waiting its turn.

**2. Unbilled energy and billing accuracy.** This finishes billing correctly. The world must generate unbilled energy the way it arises in reality, and the company must measure it. Debt and cashflow work rests on knowing what was actually billed, which is why this comes before them.

**3. Forward CLV.** Base-case cashflow and churn risk per customer — the spine every lever is judged against. Build and test it on held-back history: forecast from earlier years, check against what later years actually did.

**4. Per-customer decisions.** Continues alongside. Turning decisions that are lookup tables into per-customer choices touches acquisition, dunning and retention, so most later levers rest on it.

**5. The levers, in merit order.** Debt cashflow forecasting and negotiating tools; home moves; next best action; margin and risk; value-add — saving customers money, carbon and time, upsell and cross-sell, and EV, solar and batteries (converting customers, avoiding losses when they get the kit, generating leads for it, and giving them the ROI case).

**6. Forward simulation.** Running beyond the 2016–2025 record is wanted, but it is a hypothesis that longer runs add value. It comes after the levers deliberately: whether running forward adds anything can only be judged once there is something worth projecting forward — adoption that builds over years, debt that compounds, value-add that accumulates. Test it against real levers rather than an empty book, then build it out.

**7. Communications, sentiment and NPS.** Last. These are the hardest to simulate credibly and the most dependent on the knowledge in step 1.
