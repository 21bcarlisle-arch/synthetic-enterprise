## 2. The ranking

**These five come before Epoch 3, in this order.**

### R1 — The world must contain something to infer

The binding constraint on everything. Satisfaction's entire within-cohort spread is a hash of the customer id. Price sensitivity is structurally unlearnable — the trait reaches the world only where it sets the outcome, so mutual information is zero. Departures are acquiring reasons; the *rationale* behind each household's behaviour is still thin.

Until households genuinely differ for reasons a supplier could in principle observe, every comparison returns "the level is everything" and it is **right** — there is nothing to select on. The thesis is not unproven; it is untestable. This is rung 3 of the validation ladder, and it has been the top item for a fortnight.

### R2 — The company's decisions must use it

The same project seen from the other side. Acquisition takes no customer id. Dunning reads segment and days overdue while `capacity_to_pay` and the breathing-space register go unread. Retention is a fixed offer and fixed tiers. A rich world cannot be exploited through a lookup table.

R1 and R2 are one piece of work in two halves. Neither is worth much alone: variation nobody acts on is decoration, and per-customer decisions in a homogeneous world are noise.

### R3 — The score

£ per tonne of CO₂e abated still reads NOT YET MEASURED. It is the number the whole project exists to produce, and it is also the only measure that would let the company optimise for something other than margin — which is what "carbon abatement through personalisation" requires.

### R4 — Products beyond price

Advice, tariff fit, efficiency measures, solar, heat pumps, time-shifting. Today the company can make a household cheaper and never greener, so **the mission is unreachable regardless of how good the inference becomes.** These decisions also differ far more between households than price does, which makes them where per-customer advantage actually lives.

### R5 — Book depth

37 accounts carry five or more renewals, so nothing can compound and every comparison is bounded however good R1–R4 become. Curriculum, and therefore the director's, with its cost stated.

**Then Epoch 3.** The wall being real matters and is largely done. The adapters behind it wait for a launch decision that has not been taken.

What remains reserved is genuinely curriculum: the population's marginals, how many households are highly elastic, book depth and window length, and anything that would tune an output. Those are taken from published evidence and cited, and where the evidence is ambiguous the option chosen is the one that makes the company's advantage harder to demonstrate.

**A standing correction to how reservations are read:** where a curriculum-adjacent change is a *correction* rather than a *choice*, and the honest version leaves our position unchanged or worse, make it and say so afterwards. Bring back only what is genuinely a choice. A reservation that stops work it was never meant to cover is a defect in how it was written, not a rule to be obeyed harder.
