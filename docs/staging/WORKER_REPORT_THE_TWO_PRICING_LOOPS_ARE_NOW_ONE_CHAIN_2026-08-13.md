## The mechanism, as landed

**Producer — `simulation/run_phase2b.py`.** Each renewal opens a chain (`rate_original`,
`rate_components`, `rate_chain_entries`). Every writer now (a) takes its `unit_rate_before` from
the rate **entering that writer**, (b) appends a named component with its own sub-span, and (c)
gets `unit_rate_contracted` stamped back onto its log entry once all four have run. One
`rate_decomposition_log` entry per renewal carries `original → contracted` with the causes named
in firing order. `margin_feedback_log`'s `unit_rate_before` no longer re-reads
`term["unit_rate_gbp_per_mwh"]` — the bind that is never rebound, and the whole defect.

**Renderer — `saas/reporting/annual_report.py`.** `_rate_span_is_own` reconciles a published pair
against the coefficient printed beside it. A pair that does not reconcile is **withheld and
labelled**, never re-derived: silently recomputing the missing end would have greened the
arithmetic while hiding the producer defect, which is precisely what the finding refused. Both
tables gain a `Contracted` column, read only off the chain. The cap and the uplift are
unrecoverable from a pre-fix run, so on a legacy run `Contracted` is `—` with the reason stated
rather than a pre-cap rate published as the contracted one (R9).

**The third figure.** `Emergency` → `Margin surcharges`; `**Emergency reprices: N total**` →
`**Margin recovery surcharges: N total**`; the caption now states the trigger the mechanism
actually has — prior-term realised loss above 5% of that term's revenue, surcharge of the excess,
capped at +20% — with no cost floor claimed. `Avg Delta` states which span it covers, and spans
the whole renewal move once a run carries the chain.
