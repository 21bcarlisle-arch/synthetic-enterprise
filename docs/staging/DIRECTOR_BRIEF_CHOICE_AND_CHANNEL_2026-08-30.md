# [DIRECTOR-BRIEF] — Choice and channel: the missing mechanic (2026-08-30)

**Type:** [BRIEF — problem, evidence and requirements. Research, design, sequencing and mechanism are the delivery seat's. Nothing here prescribes an implementation.]

**Mission link.** We create enterprise value by automating ways to find individual customers we can create value for, and sharing in that value — by saving them money, time and carbon, through personalised modelling, tariffs and advice. Finding those customers, and keeping them, happens entirely inside choice and channel. Today neither exists as a mechanism.

---

## 1. The problem

A tariff type is a field. Switching is a market-wide rate. There is no competitor, no channel, no quote, and no reason attached to any departure. A household leaves because a number came up, not because anything happened to it.

The consequence is precise and it bounds everything: **the company can never be right or wrong about why a household moved.** That is the only thing worth inferring, and it is the whole of the thesis. Every A/B result to date has been measured in a world where the supplier's central commercial question cannot be asked.

**This ranks with weather and wholesale.** Those two got the full treatment — real data, published sources, a Knowledge page explaining the mechanism, and a chain from evidence into code. Choice and channel have had none of it: constants somebody picked, with nothing in the repository that could contradict them. The £150 acquisition cost sat unsourced for weeks beside a researched £55 that nothing called. That is not an accident of one number; it is what happens to an area with no knowledge layer beneath it.

---

## 2. Do it in the order that worked

Knowledge from published sources → discovery against what we have built → a roadmap. Never a constant chosen because a number was needed. The acquisition-economics work three days ago is the template: the page came first, the discovery followed, and the roadmap named six steps of which the sixth was the prize.

Establish, from real sources rather than assumption:

- How GB households actually choose an energy supply deal, and how often they do anything at all.
- What a comparison site does, what it shows, and what it charges. What the other channels are — direct, affiliate, broker, doorstep, retention — and what each costs.
- How competitors price against the cap and against each other, and what the fixed-to-SVT spread has actually done over the record.
- What triggers a household to look in the first place.

Where the published record runs out — and it will, the CMA redacts per-supplier CAC — say so rather than filling the gap with a plausible number.

---

## 3. The substance expected at the centre

**The bill payer, not the meter.** The property and the socio-economics of the household, and their needs, attitudes and behaviours. Financial predictability is a real need and it is why fixed deals exist. Apathy and inertia are real and they are why most of the book sits on the default tariff. These are the axes with genuine published heterogeneity — recall that price *weight* varies only 1.26× between subgroups while satisfaction varies 1.46× and a 17% minority prioritise exit fees. Engagement and elasticity are different things and must stay separate: a household that has never switched at any price is disengaged, not price-insensitive, and can become highly responsive the moment a bill shock makes it look.

**A reason for leaving on every departure.** End of a fixed term. Dissatisfaction. A home move onto a deemed contract. A bill shock. A better offer seen through some channel. A departure with a cause is something a supplier can predict, price and act on. A departure drawn from a rate is not, and no amount of inference skill can recover one from the other.

---

## 4. What is already established — fold it in, do not restart

Landed this week and not to be re-litigated:

- **Fixed is an active choice** for predictable, budgetable bills. **SVT arises three ways**: never engaged, lapsed off a fixed deal without acting, or moved home onto the incumbent's deemed contract.
- **Flow and stock differ.** Almost everyone who actively switches takes a fixed deal; almost nobody deliberately chooses SVT. But most accounts in a given year are not switching, so the stock is majority SVT while the flow is majority fixed. A drawn marginal models the wrong thing — generate the type from the behaviour and let the published split fall out as a check rather than an input.
- **2022 was a price effect, not scarcity.** Fixed deals were available and priced far above SVT, because a supplier pricing a year of hedged risk had to sit above a cap set on a backward-looking observation window. Households rationally declined and were mostly wrong to. The driver is the spread; generate it and let take-up follow.
- **The quoting convention matters as much as the price.** Comparison sites and the cap headline both quote an annual figure at typical consumption, which displays a variable tariff as though its current rate will hold for a year. That flatters SVT when the cap lags a rising market and flatters fixed when it lags a falling one. A household is comparing a real forward price against an implied forward price nobody is offering. **Model what the household is shown, not only what it would pay** — that is a further axis the company can be right or wrong about.
- **Acquisition is a commission paid to someone else** (PCW ~£25–30 per fuel; business is a per-kWh broker trail, not a one-off), and **retention is margin given up**, protected by SLC 22B's market-wide derogation since April 2022. Already researched, landed, and wired.
- **The SVT product in the world is approved** and sits inside this brief rather than beside it.

---

## 5. Simplify, but say what you simplified

A crude version of the whole loop is worth more than a perfect version of one part. Simplify wherever necessary — but record what was simplified, what it would take to do properly, and what it would change if done. The roadmap should tell us what is next, rather than leaving us to discover it when a result looks wrong. That is the difference between this and the constants it replaces.

---

## 6. Write it as knowledge, not only as code

Treat this as a Knowledge layer in its own right, in the same shape as the wholesale-formation, price-cap, metering and cost-stack pages: what the published evidence establishes, what it does not, what we have assumed and why, and what would settle it. Where the evidence runs out, say so on the page.

Two reasons. It is how we share the logic we are working to, rather than asking a reader to take our numbers on trust. And of all our domain areas this is the one where published understanding is thinnest and most contested — which makes it the place where the project might say something rather than merely explain something.

---

## 7. Reserved and not reserved

Curriculum values remain the director's: how many households are highly elastic, the population's marginals, anything that would tune an output. Take them from published evidence and cite them; where the evidence is ambiguous, choose the option that makes the company's advantage harder to demonstrate, record why, and move on. Erring against our own thesis is the safe direction.

Everything else here — research, design, sequencing, mechanism, what to build first — is the delivery seat's. Do not come back for permission on the shape.

## WORK THIS CREATES (canonical, in-document)
1. The knowledge: published evidence on choice, channel, competition and the quoting convention, written up as pages with their gaps stated.
2. The discovery: what our world does today against that evidence, including what already exists unwired.
3. The roadmap: sequenced, with each simplification named alongside what it would take to do properly.
4. The SVT product in the world, generated from behaviour rather than drawn as a share, with the published split used as a check.
5. A reason attached to every departure, and the bill payer's needs, attitudes and behaviours as the axes that produce it.
