## SLC 21B: reads and bills

> **21B.1** "If a Customer provides a meter reading … or if the Electricity Meter is read by the
> licensee, the licensee must take all reasonable steps to reflect the meter reading in the next
> Bill or statement of account sent to the Customer." (S1)

> **21B.4** "take all reasonable steps to obtain a meter reading (including any meter reading
> transmitted electronically from a meter to the licensee or provided by the Customer and accepted
> by the licensee) for each of its Customers at least once every year". (S1; gas S2, 21B.4(a))

- 21B.4 does not apply to prepayment meters, and took effect on 31 December 2014 (21B.3). (S1)
- It is a duty to *obtain* a read, of effort ("all reasonable steps"), not a duty to *visit*. A
  customer's own read and a smart meter's transmitted read both satisfy it.

> **21B.5** "The licensee must make available a Bill or statement of account to each of its
> Customers at least twice yearly and at least quarterly to any Customer who requests it or who has
> Online Account Management." Not prepayment, smart or unmetered supply. (S1)

## SLC 21BA: the 12-month limit on back-billing

> **21BA.1** A charge recovery action (a bill, or any other way of seeking to recover charges,
> "including via a Prepayment Meter") may recover only units "which could reasonably be considered
> to have been consumed within the 12 months preceding the date the charge recovery action was
> taken", plus standing and other supply charges accrued in that window. (S3, as made in S1)

**21BA.2, the four exceptions.** The limit does not apply where:
- (a) the charge recovery action was taken before the condition took effect;
- (b) a compliant demand was made and is being chased for non-payment;
- (c) the supplier "has been unable to take a charge recovery action for the correct amount … due to
  obstructive or manifestly unreasonable behaviour" of the customer;
- (d) anything else Ofgem specifies after consultation. None was found. (S3, S1)

**In force.** Domestic (Part A) from 1 May 2018. Microbusiness (Part B) from 1 November 2018.
(S1 21BA.9(b), S4)

**What Ofgem says is not obstruction.** "We do not consider a consumer to be obstructive or
manifestly unreasonable when he or she does not supply a meter reading." (S4 p.10) The burden of
showing an exception is the supplier's. (S4 p.9)

**What counts as a charge recovery action, beyond a bill.** Ofgem lists "accurately setting a
customer's Direct Debit payments" (S5 p.1). The Energy Ombudsman's stance, that a direct-debit
statement showing a growing balance is *not* one, is the Ombudsman's reading, not licence text. It
is recorded in `back_billing_and_liability.md` §1 and not repeated here.

## How the two conditions meet

21B.4 is a duty of effort that runs yearly. 21BA bars energy older than 12 months at the demand.
Not reading is not a 21BA exception for the supplier, and not supplying a read is not obstruction by
the customer. So a meter left unread for years costs the supplier the old energy at the first
demand, whoever's effort failed. Only 21BA.2(c) shifts that, and only on evidence of obstruction.
